# 08 — Apartment Builds 🏢

*Read [Chapter 09](../docs/09-apartment-playbook.md) first. You cannot feed
yourself from a flat — but you can produce the **fresh nutrition** that stored
calories lack, in any season, in a few square metres, with almost no power. These
are the builds that do it.*

> ⚠️ **Before you build anything on a balcony:** check your **lease and building
> rules** (plants, animals, gas cylinders, solar panels, anything fixed to the
> structure) and your **balcony's weight limit**. Wet compost is ~700 kg/m³ — a
> row of large pots is a real structural load. Keep heavy items **over the edge
> beams, close to the building wall**, never massed in the middle of a
> cantilevered slab.

# 1. Sprouting rack

**20 min** · **€20** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Build this first. It is the single most
important apartment food technique.**

Fresh, vitamin-C-rich food in **3–5 days**, from dry seed you already store, with
**no light, no soil, no power, no season and no garden.** Nothing else in this
entire plan delivers as much nutritional security per euro or per litre of
storage space.

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────┐  ← a dish tray or baking tray
        │    ╲       ╲       ╲       ╲    │    catches the drips
        │   ╔═╲═╗   ╔═╲═╗   ╔═╲═╗   ╔═╲═╗ │
        │   ║JAR║   ║JAR║   ║JAR║   ║JAR║ │  Jars at 45°, MOUTH DOWN,
        │   ║ 1 ║   ║ 2 ║   ║ 3 ║   ║ 4 ║ │  resting in a rack or a
        │   ╚═══╝   ╚═══╝   ╚═══╝   ╚═══╝ │  dish drainer
        └─────────────────────────────────┘
          Day 4    Day 3   Day 2   Day 1
          (eat)                    (soak)

        One jar started every day = a continuous daily harvest, forever.
```

**Materials:** 4 wide-mouth 1 L jars (€8) · **mesh sprouting lids** or muslin and
rubber bands (€8) · a dish drainer, a wire rack, or a wooden block with 45° holes
· a tray underneath.

**Build:** honestly, the "build" is a rack that holds four jars at 45° mouth-down
over a tray. A plastic dish drainer does it perfectly. If you want something
neater, cut four 45° notches in a piece of 45 × 95 timber and screw it to a base.

**The method**
```
1. RINSE 2–4 tbsp (30–60 g) of seed, put it in the jar.
2. SOAK 6–12 hours in plenty of cool water.
3. DRAIN COMPLETELY and stand at 45°, mouth down.
4. RINSE AND DRAIN TWICE A DAY (three times in hot weather).
   ★ Drainage is everything. Standing water is what causes spoilage. ★
5. HARVEST at 2–5 days. Rinse, drain, keep cool, eat within 3–5 days.
6. START A NEW JAR EVERY DAY.
```

**Yields (by weight):** mung bean 4–6× · alfalfa 8–10× · lentil 3–4× ·
radish/broccoli 5–6× · chickpea 3×.

⚠️ **Never sprout kidney/red beans** (toxic raw), tomato, potato, aubergine,
pepper or rhubarb seed.
⚠️ **Food safety:** sprouts grow warm and damp, which also suits *Salmonella*.
Rinse and drain properly, clean jars between batches with boiling water, and
**discard anything that smells sour, slimy or musty** — trust your nose
absolutely. If anyone in the household is pregnant, elderly, very young or
immunocompromised, **cook the sprouts** (stir-fried mung sprouts are excellent).

**Store 5 kg of sprouting seed per person** (€25–40, keeps 4+ years sealed).

# 2. Microgreen shelf with LED

**2 h** · **€100** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Fresh greens every 10 days, all year**

```
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ▬▬▬▬▬▬ LED bar, 100–150 mm above ▬▬ │ ← on adjustable chains/hooks
   │ ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 1 ▤▤▤▤   ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 2 ▤▤▤▤ │   so you can raise it
   ├─────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬ │
   │ ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 3 ▤▤▤▤   ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 4 ▤▤▤▤ │   4–6 trays on rotation
   ├─────────────────────────────────────┤   = a harvest every 2–3 days
   │ ▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬ │
   │ ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 5 ▤▤▤▤   ▤▤▤▤ TRAY 6 ▤▤▤▤ │
   └─────────────────────────────────────┘
     Each tray 200 × 300 mm, drilled, sitting in a solid tray below
```

**Materials:** a 3–4 tier shelf unit (€40, or a plank-and-bracket shelf) ·
**LED battens or grow bars, 4000–6500 K, 20–40 W per shelf** (€15–25 each) ·
chains or adjustable hooks · 12 seed trays (6 drilled + 6 solid as drip trays,
€20) · coir, compost or hemp mats · a spray bottle · a timer plug.

**Build:** mount an LED batten under each shelf, hung on chains so the height is
adjustable · put the drilled trays inside solid trays so nothing drips onto your
floor · a timer plug set to 12–16 h/day.

**Grow it**
```
Day 0     Sow 10–15 g of seed THICKLY on 20–30 mm of damp coir/compost.
Day 0–3   COVER (a tray on top, or a dark cloth). Darkness gives an even,
          upright start. Mist once a day.
Day 3     Uncover, light on, 12–16 h/day. Water from BELOW where you can —
          wet leaves in a still flat go mouldy.
Day 7–14  Harvest with scissors just above the medium. 150–250 g per tray.
          Re-sow the tray the same day.
```

**Best for substance and volume: pea shoots and sunflower shoots.** Ordinary
dried marrowfat/whole peas from the supermarket, soaked overnight and sown thick,
give a big tray of sweet shoots in 10 days and **will regrow a second cut.**
Cheapest fresh green there is. Then radish, broccoli, mustard, kale, beet,
coriander.

**Power:** 6 shelves × 30 W × 14 h = **2.5 kWh/day** if you run the lot — that is
a serious load. Options: run 2 shelves not 6 · use a sunny windowsill for half of
them and accept slower growth · run the lights only during daylight from surplus
solar. **Do the power arithmetic before you buy six lights.**

**Reuse the medium** by adding it to the wormery, then back into trays with fresh
coir mixed in.

# 3. Oyster mushroom bucket

**1 h** · **€25** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Protein and texture from waste, in the dark**

```
        ┌───────────────────────┐ ← lid with a few holes
        │  ○    ○    ○    ○     │
        │     substrate +       │  20 L bucket, 10 mm holes
        │ ○   grain spawn   ○   │  drilled in a grid at
        │     mixed through     │  ~100 mm centres all over
        │  ○    ○    ○    ○     │
        │     ○    ○    ○       │  Mushrooms fruit OUT of the holes
        └───────┬───────────────┘
             drainage holes in the base
```

**Materials:** a 20 L bucket with a lid · **oyster mushroom grain spawn**
(€10–20/kg, from a reputable supplier) · substrate: used coffee grounds, shredded
cardboard, straw, or hardwood sawdust · a drill.

**Build & run**
1. Drill **10 mm holes in a grid** over the sides of the bucket, ~100 mm apart,
   plus drainage holes in the base.
2. **Prepare the substrate:**
   - **Coffee grounds:** use within 24 h of brewing — already effectively
     pasteurised. The easiest option, and free from any café.
   - **Straw or cardboard:** soak in hot water (65–75 °C) for 1 hour, then drain
     until it is damp, not dripping. Squeeze a handful — a few drops, not a stream.
3. **Mix in 5–10% spawn by weight**, layering as you pack the bucket.
4. Lid on, **dark cupboard, 18–24 °C, 2–4 weeks.** It turns white as the mycelium
   colonises.
5. When fully white, move it somewhere with **light and humidity** — mist through
   the holes twice a day, or put the bucket inside a clear bag with slits.
6. **Mushrooms appear from the holes over 5–10 days.** Harvest by twisting the
   whole cluster off. **Expect 2–3 flushes, 200–400 g of mushrooms per kg of
   substrate.**
7. Spent substrate goes to the wormery or the balcony pots.

**Make it a cycle:** you can **propagate your own spawn** from a piece of a
mushroom stem butt laid on damp cardboard in a sealed bag. This is what turns a
purchase into a renewable system — do it while you still have healthy stock.

⚠️ **Only grow from spawn you bought from a reputable supplier.** Never eat a wild
or unknown mushroom. **Contaminant moulds (green, black, pink) mean discard the
whole bucket outdoors** — do not try to rescue it, and do not open it indoors.

# 4. Wormery

**1 h** · **€25** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Solves kitchen waste AND feeds the balcony**

With no rubbish collection, a flat fills with food waste in days. A wormery eats
it silently under the sink, produces no smell when run properly, and gives you
the compost and liquid feed your containers need.

```
        ┌───────────────────────┐ ← lid (keeps light out, flies out)
        │  ▓▓ WORKING TRAY ▓▓▓  │   add waste here
        ├───────────────────────┤
        │  ▓▓ FULL TRAY ▓▓▓▓▓▓  │   worms migrate UP to the food,
        ├───────────────────────┤   leaving finished castings behind
        │  ▓▓ FINISHED ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  │
        ├───────────────────────┤
        │   liquid collects  [TAP]  ← "worm tea": dilute 1:10, feed plants
        └───────────────────────┘
          Stackable boxes, each drilled with 5 mm holes in the BASE
```

**Materials:** 3–4 stackable plastic storage boxes (€15) · a drill · **composting
worms — *Eisenia fetida*, tiger/brandling worms** (€20 for 500 g; ⚠️ **not
earthworms from the garden, they die in a wormery**) · shredded card and coir as
bedding · a tap fitted to the bottom box (optional but useful).

**Build:** drill **5 mm holes across the base of each box** (so worms can migrate
up) and a few ventilation holes near the top of the sides · leave the bottom box
undrilled as a liquid sump, with a tap fitted low · stack them.

**Run it**
- **Bedding first:** 100 mm of damp shredded card and coir. Add the worms and
  leave them a week before feeding.
- **Feed:** vegetable and fruit peelings, coffee grounds, tea, crushed eggshells,
  shredded paper and card, spent microgreen medium.
- ❌ **Not:** meat, dairy, oily food, citrus or onion in quantity, or anything
  cooked and salty.
- **Chop it small and bury it under the surface.** Add a handful of shredded card
  with every feed — too wet and sloppy is the usual failure.
- **Feed little and often**, and only when the last lot is mostly gone. Overfeeding
  makes it sour and smelly.
- When the top box is full, **add a new box on top with fresh bedding and food**;
  the worms migrate up over a few weeks and the box below is finished castings.
- **Drain the liquid regularly, dilute it 1:10**, and use it on your containers.
  It is an excellent free feed.

**Troubleshooting:** smells → too wet or overfed; add dry card and stop feeding ·
worms escaping → too wet, too acidic, or they've run out of food · fruit flies →
bury the food properly and cover the surface with damp newspaper · too dry →
mist it.

**Site it** under the sink, in a utility cupboard, or on a shaded balcony
(bring it inside below 5 °C and above 30 °C — worms die at both extremes).

# 5. Self-watering container

**30 min** · **€10** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Halves your balcony watering**

A sub-irrigated planter has a water reservoir underneath that the compost wicks
up from. Plants take what they need, you water once or twice a week instead of
twice a day, and a missed day does not kill anything. **On a windy high balcony
this is the difference between plants that live and plants that don't.**

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
        │        COMPOST                  │
        │                                 │  ┌──┐ ← fill tube
        │   ╔═══════════════════════════╗ │  │  │   (a pipe to the bottom;
        │   ║ wicking compost column    ║ │  │  │    fill until it runs
        │  ═╩═══════════════════════════╩═│══│  │    out of the overflow)
        │  ░░░░░ RESERVOIR ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │  └──┘
        └──────────────────────────────●──┘ ← OVERFLOW HOLE at reservoir top
                                              (stops waterlogging — essential)
```

**Build from two stacked boxes or buckets:**
1. Take two identical containers, or one container plus a smaller one to sit
   inside it as a spacer.
2. Make a **platform** about 80–100 mm above the base (a perforated tray, an
   upturned pot, or a drilled plastic box) — this creates the reservoir.
3. Cut **two or three holes in the platform** and stuff them with compost or
   strips of capillary matting to form **wicks** that reach down into the water.
4. Fit a **fill tube** (20–30 mm pipe) from the top down into the reservoir.
5. ★ **Drill an overflow hole at the top of the reservoir level** ★ — this is
   the critical detail. Without it the compost waterlogs and the roots rot.
6. Fill with compost, plant, and water from the top the first time to settle it.
   Thereafter fill through the tube until it runs out of the overflow.

**Best for:** tomatoes, chillies, cucumbers, beans, leafy crops — anything that
hates drying out. **Not** for herbs like rosemary, thyme and sage, which want it
dry.

# 6. Potato sack

**15 min** · **€5** · Skill ⬤○○ · **The only real calorie crop a balcony can do**

**Build:** a 40 L woven sack, a heavy-duty bag-for-life, or a 40 L bucket with
drainage holes drilled in the base.

**Grow:**
1. **Roll the sack down** so it is only 200 mm tall. Put 100 mm of compost in.
2. Plant **2–3 chitted seed potatoes**, cover with 100 mm more compost.
3. As the shoots reach 200 mm, **unroll the sack and add more compost**, burying
   two-thirds of the stem. Repeat 2–3 times until the sack is full.
4. Water consistently — **irregular watering is what causes hollow and cracked
   tubers.** Feed with diluted worm tea or comfrey feed once the plants flower.
5. Harvest 12–16 weeks after planting, when the foliage yellows. Tip the whole
   sack onto a sheet.

**Yield: 1–2 kg per sack.** Five sacks on a sunny balcony is 5–10 kg — a week of
a family's carbohydrate. Not self-sufficiency, but real food, and children love
tipping the sack out.

**Use blight-resistant varieties** (Sarpo Mira/Axona) and fresh compost each year.

# 7. Balcony vertical growing frame

**3 h** · **€60** · Skill ⬤⬤○

Vertical space is the one dimension a flat has plenty of.

```
        ▓▓▓ wall ▓▓▓
        ▓  ┌──────────┐  ← shelf 3: herbs, salad (lightest, highest)
        ▓  ├──────────┤
        ▓  ├──────────┤  ← shelf 2: medium pots
        ▓  ├──────────┤
        ▓  └──────────┘  ← shelf 1: heavy pots, tomatoes, potato sacks
        ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓    ★ HEAVIEST AT THE BOTTOM, AGAINST THE WALL ★
        ═════════════════   over the edge beam, not the middle of the slab
```

**Build:** a ladder-style or A-frame shelf from 45 × 45 and 22 × 150 boards,
**leaning against or fixed to the wall**, with shelves stepped so each one gets
light. Or railing planters plus wall pockets plus a trellis of strings from the
railing to the ceiling for climbing beans and cucumbers.

**The rules:**
- ⚠️ **Weight at the bottom, against the building wall.** Never load the outer
  edge of a cantilevered balcony.
- ⚠️ **Secure everything against wind.** A pot falling from the fifth floor is
  lethal. Tie railing planters on with wire, not hooks. Check after every storm.
- **Wind dries pots brutally** at height — use bigger pots, mulch the surface,
  screen the windward side with mesh, and use self-watering containers (§5).
- **Follow the sun:** tallest at the north/back, shortest at the south/front.
- Check your **lease** before fixing anything to the wall or railing.

# 8. Balcony rain catch

**1 h** · **€40** · Skill ⬤○○

Even a small balcony collects usefully: **a 2 m² awning or tarp gives 1.6 litres
per millimetre of rain** — 30–80 L in a decent month. Not drinking water, but it
covers flushing, washing and watering, which is where most of your litres go.

```
        ╲╲╲╲╲ tarp or awning, sloped to one corner ╱╱╱╱╱
              ╲                                 ╱
                ╲___________________________  ╱
                          ┃  funnel/hose
                          ┃
                    ┌─────┸─────┐
                    │  water    │ ← 100–200 L butt or stacked jerry cans
                    │   butt    │   with a mesh-screened inlet
                    └────[TAP]──┘
```

**Build:** a tarp or awning tensioned with a low corner, draining through a
grommet into a hose, into a butt or jerry cans. **Mesh over every opening** —
against mosquitoes above all, which will breed in open standing water within days
and are a disease issue, not a nuisance.

**Rules:** ⚠️ **200 L = 200 kg.** Site it against the wall over the edge beam,
never mid-slab, and check the balcony's load limit. Keep it covered and dark
(algae). Treat it before any drinking use ([Water builds §1](01-water-builds.md)).
Drain it before a hard frost.

**The much bigger prize is the building's roof.** One downpipe from a flat roof
feeds a shared 1,000 L IBC in a bin store or utility room. That is a
**building-committee conversation to have in GREEN**, and it is transformative
for a whole block — see [Chapter 09 §10](../docs/09-apartment-playbook.md#10-the-building-is-your-land).

# 9. Corsi–Rosenthal air cleaner box

**30 min** · **€60** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Genuinely effective, and cheap**

A box fan and four filters taped into a cube. It moves far more clean air per euro
than most commercial purifiers, runs on ~50–80 W, and meaningfully reduces
airborne virus, smoke and dust indoors. **For a household in isolation, or with
someone ill in a sick room, this is one of the best €60 you can spend.**

```
              ┌─────────────────┐
              │   ▣▣▣ FAN ▣▣▣   │ ← box fan on top, blowing UP/OUT
              │                 │   + a cardboard shroud on the fan face
        ┌─────┴─────────────────┴─────┐   (the shroud adds 20–50% airflow —
        │ ▦▦▦ │               │ ▦▦▦   │    do not skip it)
        │ ▦F▦ │               │ ▦F▦   │
        │ ▦I▦ │   air drawn   │ ▦I▦   │  4 filters forming the sides,
        │ ▦L▦ │   IN through  │ ▦L▦   │  arrows pointing INWARD
        │ ▦T▦ │   all 4 sides │ ▦T▦   │
        │ ▦E▦ │               │ ▦E▦   │
        │ ▦R▦ │               │ ▦R▦   │
        └─────┴───────────────┴───────┘
                cardboard base, fully taped — no air leaks
```

**Materials:** **4 filters, MERV-13 or European F7 / ePM1 50–65%**, 500 × 500 mm
(or 20" × 20"), 50 mm deep · a box fan of the same face size · stiff cardboard
for the base and the shroud · **a lot of duct tape.**

**Build**
1. Tape the four filters into a cube, **arrows on the filter frames all pointing
   INWARD** (air flows in through the filters, out through the fan).
2. Tape a cardboard base on the bottom. **Seal every seam** — any leak is
   unfiltered air bypassing the whole thing.
3. Sit the fan on top, blowing **outward/upward**, and tape it down all round.
4. **Cut a cardboard shroud** — a square of card covering the fan's face with a
   circular hole cut to match the fan blade circle — and tape it over the fan
   outlet. This one piece of cardboard adds 20–50% to the airflow by stopping
   recirculation at the blade tips.

**Use it:** run it on **low or medium** — it is quieter and barely less effective
per watt · site it in the middle of the room, not against a wall · **one in the
shared living space and one in the sick room** · filters last 6–12 months in
normal use, less in smoke; buy spares.

**In Europe**, 20" box fans can be hard to find. Alternatives: a smaller 4-filter
cube using 400 × 400 filters with a suitable fan · a **single-filter "bucket"
design** with a PC fan array · or simply a commercial HEPA unit if you can get
one. The principle — **a lot of filter area and a modest fan** — is what matters,
not the exact format.

**Pair it with a CO₂ meter** ([Chapter 06](../docs/06-health-and-pandemic-protocol.md)):
a filter cleans the air, but only ventilation removes CO₂. Below 800 ppm means
you are actually exchanging air. Use both together.

# 10. Under-bed storage system

**2 h** · **€60** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Where a year of food actually goes**

A year of staples for four is roughly **965 kg and 1.5–2 m³.** A flat has that
space; it is just distributed in awkward places.

```
   Standard double bed: 2000 × 1400 mm of floor, 200–300 mm of clearance
   = 0.6–0.8 m³ of storage = 250–350 kg of dry staples UNDER ONE BED
```

**Build:** low flat boxes or drawers on **castors** (so they roll out — anything
you have to crawl for, you will not rotate), 200 mm high, sized to your clearance.
Ply, or plastic storage boxes with lids. Bed risers (€20) add another 100–150 mm
of clearance and 50% more capacity.

**The rules:**
- **Raise everything off the floor** on battens or a ply base — floors are cold,
  they sweat, and a burst pipe or a spilled drink ruins a season's food.
- **Keep it cool and dark.** Under a bed on an internal wall is good; next to a
  radiator is the worst place in the flat. **Shelf life roughly halves for every
  +10 °C.**
- **Mylar and buckets, or glass jars with O₂ absorbers.** Jars are rodent-proof
  and look like a pantry rather than a stockpile ([OPSEC](../docs/10-security-community-communications.md)).
- **Label the top of every box** and keep the written inventory current
  ([inventory template](../worksheets/inventory-template.csv)). Storage you
  cannot see is storage you will not rotate.
- **Heaviest at the bottom, densest calories first:** oil, nuts, pasta, rice,
  sugar, honey, powdered milk, tinned fish, peanut butter.

**The other spaces:** wardrobe bases · above kitchen units (to the ceiling) ·
behind and under the sofa · hallway cupboard · the space above internal doors ·
a shelf around the top of a room · a cellar locker if the building has one · a
rented storage unit for the L3 bulk layer.

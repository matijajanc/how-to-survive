# Build Plans

*Construction plans for everything in this survival plan that you can make
yourself. Each build gives dimensions, a materials list, a cut list, step-by-step
assembly, cost, time and the mistakes that ruin it.*

**Every one of these can be built with hand tools by someone who has never built
anything.** Where a build genuinely needs skill or a professional (a wood stove
flue, a mains transfer switch, a structural cellar), it says so plainly.

## Before you start: the fundamentals

### The tool kit that builds everything here

**Absolute minimum (€120):** tape measure · pencil · combination square · handsaw ·
hammer · cordless drill/driver + bits · adjustable spanner · utility knife ·
tin snips · 2 clamps · spirit level · safety glasses · work gloves.

**Makes it much faster (€200 more):** circular saw or mitre saw · jigsaw ·
impact driver · staple gun · long spirit level (1.2 m) · workbench or two
trestles · a chalk line · a spade and a shovel.

**Chapter 11** covers the full tool and spares list.

### Timber sizes used in these plans

European metric sawn timber. If yours differs, adapt — none of these designs are
fussy about a few millimetres.

| Name here | Actual size | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **45 × 45** | 45 × 45 mm (2×2) | Frames, studs, coop structure, small builds |
| **45 × 95** | 45 × 95 mm (2×4) | Load-bearing frames, bed edges, stands, run posts |
| **70 × 70** | 70 × 70 mm (3×3) | Legs, posts |
| **22 × 100** | 22 × 100 mm board | Cladding, bed edges, shelving |
| **Batten** | 25 × 38 mm | Roofing battens, glazing bars, trim |
| **Sheet** | 2440 × 1220 mm | OSB3, exterior ply, MDF |

**Sheet goods:** OSB3 (moisture-resistant, cheap, ugly) for anything that will be
painted or covered · **exterior/marine plywood** for floors, doors and anything
that gets wet or scrubbed · 18 mm for structure, 12 mm for panels, 6 mm for
linings.

### Timber preservation and food safety

- **Never use CCA-treated, creosoted or old railway-sleeper timber** where animals
  live, where food grows, or where water is collected. The preservatives leach.
- Modern **tanalised/pressure-treated (ACQ, copper azole)** timber is generally
  considered safe for raised beds and animal housing, but line the inside face of
  raised beds with plastic sheeting if you want to be conservative.
- **Best untreated options** that last outdoors: larch, Douglas fir, sweet
  chestnut, oak, western red cedar, black locust. Larch is usually the cheapest
  durable option in Central Europe and needs no treatment at all.
- **Raw linseed oil** (2–3 coats, thinned) is a non-toxic finish for coops, beds
  and tool handles.
- **Keep wood off the ground.** A post in soil rots in 5–8 years; the same post on
  a concrete pad or a metal post shoe lasts 25.

### Fixings

- **Screws, not nails**, for almost everything — they can be undone, they hold
  better, and you can build alone. Exterior-grade (coated or stainless) outdoors.
- Rule of thumb: the screw should go **at least twice as deep** into the second
  piece as the first piece is thick.
- **Pre-drill near ends** to stop splitting (a pilot hole ≈ the screw's core
  diameter).
- **Galvanised or stainless outdoors.** Plain steel bleeds rust stains within a
  season and fails within five years.
- **Structural connections:** use a bolt (M8/M10 with washers), not a screw.
- **Coach screws** for post-to-rail, **angle brackets** for anything you'd
  otherwise butt-joint badly.

### Mesh — the one you must not get wrong

| Mesh | Use | ⚠️ |
|---|---|---|
| **12.5 mm (½") galvanised weld mesh, 19 g** | **Everything predator-related: coop vents, run walls, aprons, feed stores** | The correct answer |
| 25 mm (1") weld mesh | Run tops, upper sections, rabbit runs | Acceptable above 1.2 m |
| Chicken wire (hexagonal netting) | **Keeping chickens in. Nothing else.** | ❌ **A fox tears through it and a marten walks through it. Never use it as predator protection.** |
| Insect mesh (0.6–1 mm) | Brassica cages, window screens | |
| Bird netting | Fruit cages, run tops against hawks | |
| Hardware cloth 6 mm | Rodent-proofing feed stores, vents | |

### Safety

- **Eye protection** whenever cutting, drilling or hammering. Every time.
- **Dust mask (FFP2)** when cutting OSB, MDF or treated timber — the dust is
  genuinely harmful.
- **Never cut towards yourself.** Clamp the work; don't hold it.
- **Ladders:** 1-in-4 angle, three points of contact, someone footing it.
- **Chainsaw:** full PPE and training, never alone, never above shoulder height.
- **Anything burning:** read the CO warning in [Chapter 04 §6](../docs/04-heating-cooking-fuel.md#6-carbon-monoxide-the-thing-that-will-actually-kill-you). It applies to every stove
  in these plans.
- **Anything electrical touching house wiring:** a qualified electrician. Not you.
- **Anything holding water above head height:** 1,000 L weighs 1,000 kg. Build
  the stand to hold **three times** what you think it needs.
- **Excavations over 1.2 m deep** can collapse and kill. Batter the sides or
  shore them. This is the most dangerous build in this whole folder.

### Adapt, scavenge, substitute

Almost everything here can be built from salvage: pallets (heat-treated "HT"
stamped only — **never MB/methyl bromide**), old windows, scaffold boards,
IBC totes, blue barrels, bread crates, scrap timber, offcuts from a building
site, a demolished shed. **The dimensions in these plans are a starting point,
not a law.** Build to the materials you can actually get.

Keep every offcut. In a crisis the timber pile is a resource.

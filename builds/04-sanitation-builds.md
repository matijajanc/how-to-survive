# 1. Bucket toilet (sawdust toilet)

**2 h** · **€40** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Build this in your first weekend**

Odour-free, fly-free, usable indoors, and it works whether you are in a
farmhouse or on the ninth floor. Done properly it smells of nothing but sawdust.
Done badly it is unbearable — and the difference is one habit.

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────┐
        │ ╔═══════════════════════╗   │ ← standard toilet seat + LID,
        │ ║      SEAT OPENING     ║   │   screwed to the top
        │ ╚═══════════════════════╝   │
        ├─────────────────────────────┤
        │      ┌───────────────┐      │  Box: 500 W × 450 D × 430 H
        │      │               │      │  (430 = normal seat height)
        │      │    20 L       │      │
        │      │   BUCKET      │      │  Front or back panel hinged/lift-off
        │      │               │      │  so the bucket lifts straight out
        │      └───────────────┘      │
        └─────────────────────────────┘
              beside it: a bin of COVER MATERIAL and a scoop
```

**Materials**
| Item | Qty | ~€ |
|---|---|---|
| 18 mm plywood or OSB (one half-sheet) | — | 20 |
| 45 × 45 corner battens | 3 m | 5 |
| Standard toilet seat with lid | 1 | 15 |
| **20 L buckets** (at least 2 — one in use, one in reserve) | 2–4 | 16 |
| Screws, hinges or a turn-button | — | 5 |
| Cover material bin + scoop | 1 | 10 |

**Build**
1. Cut the ply: top 500 × 450 · two sides 450 × 430 · back 500 × 430 · front
   500 × 430 (**make the front lift off or hinge** — this is how you change the
   bucket).
2. Batten the corners inside and screw the box together.
3. **Measure your actual bucket rim** and cut the hole in the top so the bucket
   rim sits just under the seat, catching everything. A hole that is too big lets
   things miss the bucket; too small and the seat overhangs. Cut it 10 mm smaller
   than the bucket rim diameter.
4. Screw the toilet seat over the hole.
5. Sand, and finish with paint or oil so it wipes clean.
6. Put a **large bin of cover material and a scoop right beside it.** If the
   cover material is not within arm's reach, people will not use it, and then it
   stinks.

**The method — this is the whole thing**
```
1. Start every empty bucket with 30–50 mm of cover material in the bottom.
2. Use it normally. Urine and faeces together is fine (but see §2).
3. ★ COVER COMPLETELY AFTER EVERY SINGLE USE ★
   A generous scoop, until nothing is visible. This is the entire trick.
   A covered deposit does not smell and does not attract flies.
4. Lid down between uses.
5. Swap the bucket at two-thirds full. Never carry a full one.
6. Rinse the empty bucket, tip the rinse water on the compost pile, air it.
```

**Cover material** — you need a lot, so plan it: sawdust or shavings (untreated
wood only) · **coconut coir bricks** (one 5 kg brick expands to ~70 L — the
apartment answer, compact to store) · shredded dry leaves · shredded paper and
card · peat-free compost · chopped straw · dry soil · rice hulls.
**Budget 1–2 L per person per day → ~150–250 L per person per three months.**

⚠️ Never use cat litter (clay clumps and won't compost), and never use sawdust
from treated, painted or MDF timber.

# 2. Urine diverter

**1 h** · **€0–80** · Skill ⬤○○ · **The upgrade that changes everything**

Urine is **~90% of the volume** and almost all of the smell once mixed with
faeces. Separating it means the solids bucket fills **five times more slowly**,
barely smells at all, needs far less cover material, and gives you a free
nitrogen fertiliser.

**Three ways, cheapest first:**
1. **A dedicated bottle or jug** for men and children, used before sitting.
   Free, and it captures most of the volume immediately.
2. **A funnel and tube** fitted at the front of the seat opening, draining to a
   5 L jerry can beside the box. €10 of parts.
3. **A commercial urine-diverting seat or insert** (€40–80). The comfortable,
   reliable option, and worth it if this is going to run for months.

**What to do with the urine**
- **Dilute 1:8 to 1:10 with water** and pour onto **soil** around growing plants.
  Not on leaves, not on anything about to be eaten raw, and stop 3–4 weeks before
  harvest.
- One adult's annual urine contains roughly enough nitrogen for **200–300 m² of
  vegetable garden** — a serious free input when fertiliser cannot be bought.
- 🏢 **In a flat:** dilute and pour down a still-working drain, or onto balcony
  pots and the trees below (ask first). Undiluted urine burns plants and stinks
  within hours — **dilute it and use it the same day.**
- Don't use urine from anyone on medication you would rather not concentrate in
  your soil.

# 3. Two-bay humanure compost system

**4 h** · **€40 (free from pallets)** · Skill ⬤○○ · 🏡 **land only**

```
      ┌─────────────┬─────────────┐
      │             │             │   Two bays, 1 m × 1 m × 1 m each
      │   BAY A     │   BAY B     │
      │  FILLING    │  RESTING    │   Fill A for ONE YEAR.
      │  this year  │  (sealed,   │   Then seal it and fill B for a year.
      │             │   year 2)   │   Two years total before use.
      │             │             │
      └─────────────┴─────────────┘   Front boards slide out for access
       ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓
       300 mm base of straw / wood chips to absorb all liquid
```

**Siting — get this right first**
- **At least 30 m from any well, spring, stream or watercourse, and downhill
  from your water source.** Non-negotiable.
- Out of sight, downwind of the house, but close enough that you will actually
  walk there twice a day in February.
- **On soil, never on concrete** — the soil biology and the drainage are part of
  the process.

**Build:** four pallets (or 22 × 100 boards on 70 × 70 posts) forming two
adjoining 1 m³ bays, with **removable front boards** sliding in grooves. Line the
inside with cardboard if the pallet gaps are large. A simple lid or a sheet of
corrugated tin keeps the rain off (but it must not be sealed — this is an aerobic
process and it needs air).

**Operate it**
1. **Start with a 300 mm base of straw or wood chips** in the bay. This is the
   "biological sponge" — it absorbs every drop of liquid so nothing leaches out.
2. **Empty the bucket into the centre of the pile**, then **immediately cover it
   completely** with straw, leaves or chips. Never leave deposited material
   uncovered, even for an hour.
3. Add kitchen waste, garden waste, weeds and animal bedding to the same pile —
   they add carbon, nitrogen and biological diversity and help it heat.
4. Keep the sides and top well covered with a thick carbon layer. **A correctly
   managed humanure pile has no odour and no flies.** If it smells, add more cover.
5. **Fill one bay for a full year. Then seal it and leave it completely
   undisturbed for a second full year** while you fill the other.
6. A hot pile reaching 50–60 °C kills pathogens in days, but a pile built from
   small daily additions in cold weather often will not get there. **The long
   rest is the real safety margin, not the temperature.** Do not shorten it.

**Using the finished compost**
- ✅ Fruit trees, berry bushes, ornamentals, hedges, grain and fodder crops,
  and building soil in new beds.
- ❌ **Not on root vegetables, salads, or anything eaten raw.**
- Be conservative. Caution here costs you nothing.

**Hygiene:** dedicated gloves and boots for this job, a brush at the door, and
hands washed at the tippy-tap on the way back. Every time.

# 4. Plumbed handwash station

**1 h** · **€25** · Skill ⬤○○

The [tippy-tap](01-water-builds.md#2-tippy-tap-handwashing-station) is the free
version. This is the permanent indoor/outdoor one.

**Build:** a **20 L jerry can with a tap**, mounted on a shelf or bracket at
about 1.1 m, over a basin that drains into a bucket. Soap on a rope beside it,
and a nail for a towel.

**Details:**
- **Site one at the toilet and one at the kitchen.** Handwashing at the point of
  need is what works; a sink in another room is not used.
- Refilling it is a named daily job on the rota.
- Catch the greywater in a bucket underneath and put it on the fruit trees or use
  it for flushing.
- A **foot-operated tap** (a lever pressing the tap arm) is better still — no
  hand contact with the tap at all, which is the whole point.
- **Warm water doubles compliance in winter.** A jug of hot water from the stove
  into the jerry can once a day makes the difference between people washing and
  people not washing.

# 5. Bucket plunger washing machine + wringer

**1 h** · **€35** · Skill ⬤○○

Hand-washing a family's laundry with a plunger is hard work; with a plunger and a
wringer it is a manageable Monday morning.

```
     WASH               RINSE            WRING
   ┌────────┐        ┌────────┐      ╔═══════════╗
   │ ║║║║║  │        │ ║║║║║  │      ║  mop      ║
   │ ║plunger        │        │      ║  wringer  ║ ← or a hand mangle
   │ ║║║║║  │        │        │      ║  bucket   ║
   │ hot +  │        │  cool  │      ╚═══════════╝
   │  soap  │        │  clean │       drips back into the rinse bucket
   └────────┘        └────────┘
   20 L bucket       20 L bucket
   with a lid,       reuse this water as
   hole cut for      TOMORROW'S wash water
   the handle
```

**Materials:** 2 × 20 L buckets · a bucket lid with a 40 mm hole cut in the
centre · a **"breathing mobile washer"** plunger (€25) or a clean toilet plunger
with six 20 mm holes drilled through the cup · a **mop bucket wringer** (€20) or
a hand mangle · a washboard (€15, genuinely useful for collars and cuffs).

**Method**
1. Wash bucket: hot water, soap or washing soda, and **no more than 3–4 garments**
   at a time. Overloading is why hand washing feels impossible.
2. Lid on with the plunger handle through the hole (stops it splashing everywhere
   and keeps the heat in). **Plunge 2–3 minutes.**
3. Soak 20 minutes, plunge again for 1 minute.
4. Wring, transfer to the rinse bucket, plunge 1 minute, wring again.
5. **Reuse the rinse water as the next load's wash water**, and the final
   greywater on the garden. A family wash costs about 30 L this way instead of 150.

**Drying is the real bottleneck in winter.** A wringer or mangle removes far more
water than hands can, which can halve the drying time. Dry in the warm room
**with the window cracked** — wet laundry in a sealed room causes condensation,
mould and respiratory problems. A rack over the stove, or a line in the loft
above the warm room, works best.

# 6. Greywater mulch basin

**2 h** · **€20** · Skill ⬤○○ · 🏡

Turns washing water into irrigation without pipes, filters or pumps.

```
        greywater bucket or pipe
                 │
                 ▼
        ┌─────────────────────────┐  ← 300 mm deep, 1 m across
        │ ░░░ wood chips / straw ░│    basin around a FRUIT TREE
        │ ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │    (not vegetables)
        │ ░░░░  ░░░░  ░░░░  ░░░░  │    Filled with coarse mulch, which
        └─────────────────────────┘    filters, absorbs and feeds the soil
                 tree
```

**Build:** dig a shallow basin 300 mm deep and about 1 m across around a fruit
tree or berry bush, **outside the trunk's drip zone** (never against the trunk —
that causes collar rot). Fill it with coarse wood chips or straw. Pour or pipe
greywater into it. Top up the mulch twice a year as it breaks down.

**Rules**
- **Use it within 24 hours.** Stored greywater goes anaerobic and foul fast.
- **Plant-safe soap only:** no boron, no chlorine bleach, low sodium. Simple soap
  and potash soaps are fine; most laundry powders are not.
- **Apply to soil under mulch, never to foliage**, and **never to root crops or
  anything eaten raw.**
- Fruit trees, berry bushes, sweetcorn, potatoes and ornamentals are ideal targets.
- Never let it pond or run off. Keep it away from the water source and from where
  children play.
- Kitchen sink water with grease and food scraps clogs quickly — screen it first,
  or send that one to the compost heap instead.

# 7. Trench latrine

**1 h** · **€0** · Skill ⬤○○ · 🏡 **short-term only**

For a large group, a temporary camp, or when the bucket system is overwhelmed.

**Rules:**
- **At least 30 m from any water source, and downhill from it.**
- **300 mm wide, 1 m deep**, as long as needed. Pile the spoil beside it with a
  spade stuck in.
- A log or a simple frame to sit on, and a screen for privacy.
- **A scoop of soil and wood ash after every use.**
- Backfill and mound over when it is **300 mm from the top**, and mark it. Do not
  fill to the brim.
- Not usable in frozen ground or where the water table is high.
- Handwashing station at the exit, always.

⚠️ A trench latrine is a short-term measure. For anything lasting more than a few
weeks, the covered bucket system with proper composting is cleaner, safer, closer
to the house, works in winter, and returns fertility instead of burying it.

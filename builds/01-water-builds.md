# 1. Bucket gravity filter

**30 min** · **€50** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Build this first. Today if possible.**

Two stacked buckets and a hollow-fibre filter give you a continuous, hands-free
supply of safe water at 1–2 litres a minute, with no power and no pumping. It
does the same job as a €300 commercial gravity filter for a sixth of the price.

```
        ┌───────────────────┐ ← lid (keeps dust and insects out)
        │  ░░░ RAW WATER ░░ │
        │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │  TOP BUCKET — 20 L, drilled for the filter
        └────────╥──────────┘
                 ║ ← Sawyer/hollow-fibre filter, gasket + washer both sides
        ┌────────╨──────────┐
        │                   │
        │   CLEAN WATER     │  BOTTOM BUCKET — 20 L, with a tap 50 mm up
        │                   │
        └──────────────[TAP]┘
             ═══════════════   stand on a stable shelf at working height
```

**Materials**
| Item | Qty | ~€ |
|---|---|---|
| Food-grade 20 L buckets with lids | 2 | 16 |
| **Sawyer-type hollow-fibre filter (0.1 µm)** with gaskets | 1–2 | 30 |
| Plastic bucket tap (spigot) + washers | 1 | 6 |
| Silicone sealant (food-safe) | — | 4 |

**Build**
1. Drill a hole in the **centre of the base of the top bucket** to suit your
   filter's threaded fitting (usually 19–20 mm).
2. Drill a matching hole in the **lid of the bottom bucket** — or omit the lid and
   let the filter hang straight into the bottom bucket.
3. Fit the filter through both, with a rubber gasket and washer on **each** side,
   and tighten the nut hand-tight plus a quarter turn. Do not over-tighten —
   cracked plastic is the usual failure.
4. Fit the tap into the bottom bucket, **50 mm up from the base**, with washers
   inside and out. The gap leaves any sediment behind.
5. Run a bead of silicone around both fittings; let it cure 24 h.
6. **Test with clean water first**, and look for drips. Any leak at the filter
   seal means untreated water is bypassing the filter.

**Use it**
- **Two filters double the flow rate.** For a family of four, fit two.
- Pre-filter cloudy water through 4–8 layers of cotton, or settle it first —
  silt clogs the fibres fast.
- **Backflush weekly** with the syringe that comes with the filter, and whenever
  flow drops.
- ⚠️ **Filtration is not disinfection.** Hollow-fibre removes bacteria and
  protozoa but **not viruses**. After filtering, add a NaDCC tablet or 2 drops of
  bleach per litre, or boil, if the source was surface water. From your own
  rainwater tank, filter + chlorine residual is the sensible standard.
- ⚠️ **Never let the filter freeze.** Freezing ruptures the fibres invisibly and
  it will then pass everything without any change you can see. Bring it indoors
  in winter.

# 2. Tippy-tap handwashing station

**20 min** · **€5** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Highest disease-prevention per euro in the
whole folder**

Handwashing with soap cuts diarrhoeal disease by 30–50%. A tippy-tap uses
**0.2 litres per wash**, and — the important part — you never touch it with dirty
hands.

```
        ┌───────┐          Two uprights + a crossbar
       ╱│ 5 L   │╲         (or hang it from a branch or a nail)
      ╱ │  jug  │ ╲
     │  └───┬───┘  │        Jug pivots on a string through two
     │      │      │        holes near the neck
     │     ╱ ╲     │
     │    ╱   ╲    │  ← 3 mm hole in the cap or upper shoulder
     │   soap on   │    lets water out ONLY when tilted
     │   a string  │
     │      │      │
     │   ───┴───   │  ← foot lever, string to the jug's handle
    ═╧═════════════╧═
      gravel or a soakaway below, or a bucket to catch greywater
```

**Build**
1. Take a clean 5 L jug or bottle with a handle.
2. Drill a **3 mm hole** in the cap (or in the shoulder, 30 mm below the neck).
3. Drill two small holes either side of the neck and thread a string through as a
   pivot; hang it from a crossbar so it swings freely.
4. Tie a second string from the handle down to a foot lever (a stick pivoted on a
   peg) or simply a loop you pull with your foot.
5. Hang a bar of soap in a net or on a nail beside it — **soap is what does the
   work**; running water alone does very little.
6. Put gravel or a bucket underneath. Catch the greywater for the garden.

**Site one at the toilet and one at the kitchen.** Refill them as a daily job.
In winter, bring the jug indoors at night or it splits.

# 3. First-flush diverter

**1 h** · **€25** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Do more for water quality than any filter**

The first rain off a roof carries the bird droppings, dust, pollen, moss and
insects that accumulated since the last shower. A first-flush diverter throws
that away and sends only the clean water to the tank.

```
             roof gutter
                 │
                 ▼
            ┌────┴────┐
            │  tee    ├──────────► to TANK (once the chamber is full)
            └────┬────┘
                 │
            ┌────┴────┐  ← ball floats up as the chamber fills
            │    ●    │     and seals the tee, diverting flow onward
            │         │
            │ 110 mm  │     CHAMBER — volume = roof area × 0.5 L/m²
            │  pipe   │
            │         │     110 mm pipe holds ~8.5 L per metre
            │         │     160 mm pipe holds ~18 L per metre
            └────┬────┘
              ┌──┴──┐  ← end cap with a 3 mm hole:
              │  •  │     drains slowly between rain events so the
              └─────┘     chamber is empty and ready for the next shower
```

**Sizing.** Aim for **0.5 litres per m² of roof area**:
| Roof area | First flush volume | 110 mm pipe | 160 mm pipe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 m² | 20 L | 2.4 m | 1.1 m |
| 60 m² | 30 L | 3.5 m | 1.7 m |
| 100 m² | 50 L | 5.9 m | 2.8 m |

> Most DIY diverters are undersized because a 6 m pipe is impractical. **An
> undersized diverter is still far better than none** — 20 L takes the worst of
> it. Use 160 mm pipe, or split the roof across two downpipes with a diverter on
> each, or accept a smaller chamber and clean your gutters more often.

**Materials:** 110 or 160 mm soil pipe to the length above · a tee · an end cap ·
2 pipe clips · a floating ball that fits the pipe loosely (a hard plastic ball,
or a sealed empty bottle) · solvent cement or rubber seals.

**Build**
1. Cut the downpipe and fit the tee so the **side outlet goes to the tank** and
   the **straight run goes down into the chamber**.
2. Glue the chamber pipe below the tee, clipped to the wall.
3. Drop the ball in. As the chamber fills, the ball rises and seals the opening,
   so further water passes out of the side outlet to the tank.
4. Fit the end cap and drill a **3 mm hole** in it. The chamber empties over a few
   hours, ready for the next shower.
5. Add a **leaf screen / 1 mm mesh basket** at the gutter outlet above the tee.
   It keeps out leaves, and — critically — **mosquitoes and rodents**, which is a
   disease issue, not a tidiness one.

**Maintenance:** clear the mesh monthly and after storms. Flush the chamber and
check the drain hole twice a year. Disconnect and drain it before a hard frost.

# 4. IBC tank stand and linked manifold

**4 h** · **€80** · Skill ⬤⬤○

⚠️ **A full 1,000 L IBC weighs 1,000 kg.** Build the stand for three times that.
A collapsing tank will destroy a wall, a car or a person.

```
    TWO LINKED IBCs, plan view                SIDE VIEW
   ┌──────────┐   ┌──────────┐              ┌────────────┐
   │  IBC 1   │   │  IBC 2   │              │    IBC     │
   │  1000 L  │   │  1000 L  │              │            │
   └────╥─────┘   └─────╥────┘              └──╥─────────┘
        ╚════ 50 mm ════╝  ← linked at the      ║ outlet
             manifold        BASE: they fill  ══╬══════  ← 45 × 95 bearers
                             and draw as one   ███  ███  ← concrete blocks
                  │                            ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  ← compacted hardcore
              ┌───┴───┐                                     on level ground
              │  TAP  │ ← 500–600 mm above ground
              └───────┘   gives enough head for a hose and to fill a can
```

**Source the tanks carefully.** Buy only **food-grade IBCs that previously held
food** (syrup, juice, glycerine). Ask what was in it and do not accept "not
sure". A tank that held a chemical can never be made safe for drinking water.
Rinse thoroughly before use regardless.

**Build the stand**
1. **Level and compact the ground.** Everything depends on this. Dig out 150 mm,
   fill with hardcore/gravel, compact it hard, and check level in both directions.
2. Lay **concrete blocks on edge** (or paving slabs on a full bed) to 400–500 mm
   high, supporting **the full perimeter of the IBC pallet base**, not just the
   corners.
3. Lay **45 × 95 bearers** across the blocks, running the same way as the pallet's
   own bearers, so the load goes straight down into the blocks.
4. Check level again with the tank empty, and again after the first fill.

**Link the tanks**
5. Connect the **bottom outlets** together with 50 mm pipe and the standard IBC
   camlock/S60x6 adaptors (cheap, available online). Linked at the base, the tanks
   equalise — they fill and empty together and you draw from one tap.
6. **Link the tops too** with a short overflow pipe, and take a final overflow
   away from the house foundations to a soakaway, a ditch or a pond.
7. Fit an isolating valve at each tank so you can take one offline to clean it.

**Finish**
8. **Block the light** — paint the tanks, wrap them in tarpaulin, or build a
   simple timber surround. **Sunlight grows algae**, and algae fouls filters and
   makes the water taste foul.
9. Fit a **tap 100 mm above the tank floor** so sediment stays behind, and a
   separate bottom drain to flush the sludge out twice a year.
10. **Winter:** drain the pipework and the tap, leave the tanks no more than
    ¾ full, or lag them. Water expands as it freezes and will split a tank.
    Partially burying a tank, or surrounding it with straw bales, keeps it liquid.

**Insulating one tank** (straw bales or 50 mm foam board in a timber box) means
you still have liquid water in January, which is when you need it most.

# 5. Slow sand (biosand) filter

**4 h to build + 2–3 weeks to mature** · **€40** · Skill ⬤⬤○

A genuinely effective, permanent, zero-consumable filter. Once the **biolayer**
(a living film of micro-organisms on the sand surface) has matured, it removes
95–99% of bacteria, over 99% of protozoa, and most turbidity — indefinitely, with
no cartridges to buy. This is a real L3 renewable.

```
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │   ▓▓ diffuser plate ▓▓  │ ← drilled plate, 50 mm above the sand:
        │═════════════════════════│   pouring water straight onto the sand
        │  ~~ 50 mm standing ~~   │   destroys the biolayer
        │  ~~~~~ water ~~~~~~~~~  │
        │▪▪▪▪ BIOLAYER ▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪ │ ← the living filter, top 10 mm
        │░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │
        │░ FILTRATION SAND        │   500 mm of 0.15–0.7 mm washed sand
        │░ 500 mm                 │
        │░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │
        │▒▒ coarse sand 50 mm ▒▒▒ │   1–6 mm
        │●● drainage gravel 50 ●● │   6–12 mm
        └──┬──────────────────────┘
           │ outlet pipe rises from the base...
           └────────────┐
                        └──► outlet at the level of the standing water
                             (this is what keeps 50 mm of water permanently
                              above the sand, keeping the biolayer alive)
```

**Materials:** a 60–120 L food-grade barrel or a purpose-built concrete body ·
20 mm pipe and elbows · **washed sand, graded** · gravel · a drilled plastic
plate for the diffuser.

**Build**
1. Fit the **outlet pipe**: from the very bottom of the barrel, rising outside (or
   inside) to discharge at a height that leaves **50 mm of water standing above
   the sand surface**. Getting this height right is the whole design — it is what
   keeps the biolayer permanently wet.
2. **Wash the sand until the rinse water runs completely clear.** This takes
   longer than you think — an hour of work, several changes of water. Unwashed
   sand clogs the filter and it never recovers. Do not skip it.
3. Layer in: 50 mm of 6–12 mm gravel, 50 mm of 1–6 mm coarse sand, then **500 mm
   of 0.15–0.7 mm filtration sand**.
4. Fit the diffuser plate 50 mm above the sand.
5. **Charge it daily with water for 2–3 weeks** before trusting it. The biolayer
   has to establish. Flow will slow as it matures — that is the filter working.

**Operate it**
- **Pour a batch (10–20 L) through, once or twice a day.** It is a batch filter,
  not a continuous one. The pause between batches is when the biolayer feeds.
- Flow rate of roughly **0.4–0.6 L/min** is right.
- ⚠️ **Never let it dry out.** A dry biolayer is a dead biolayer and you start the
  three weeks again.
- ⚠️ **Never let it freeze.**
- When flow drops too far, do the **"swirl and dump"**: stir the top 20 mm of
  sand gently, let the dirt suspend, scoop the dirty water out. Expect 1–2 weeks
  of reduced performance while the biolayer recovers.
- ⚠️ **Still disinfect afterwards.** A biosand filter is excellent but it is not
  a virus barrier. Chlorinate or boil the output for drinking.

# 6. Solar batch water heater

**1 day** · **€60** · Skill ⬤⬤○ · **Free hot water, April–September**

## 6.1 The simple version — solar shower bag

A 20 L black PVC bag (€15) hung on a sunny south wall reaches **40 °C in 3–4
hours** on a clear day. Zero build. Buy two. This is the sensible starting point
and it is genuinely enough for washing.

## 6.2 The batch heater — 100–200 L of hot water

```
             ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
       glass │  ╔════════════════════════════════╗  │ ← old window or
        lid  │  ║   BLACK-PAINTED TANK / BARREL  ║  │   twin-wall polycarbonate
       (30°) │  ╚════════════════════════════════╝  │
             │  ▓▓▓ 50 mm insulation all round ▓▓▓  │ ← except the glazed face
             └──────────────────────────────────────┘
              cold in at the BOTTOM · hot out at the TOP
              (hot water rises — never plumb it the other way)
```

**Build:** a plywood box with 50 mm of insulation on the base and all sides, a
barrel or steel tank **painted matt black** inside it, and an old double-glazed
window or twin-wall polycarbonate as the lid, tilted to face the sun at about
your latitude. Line the inside of the box with foil or white paint to bounce
light back onto the tank.

**Plumb it:** cold feed into the **bottom**, hot draw-off from the **top**. On a
clear Central European summer day expect **45–60 °C**; in April and September,
30–40 °C — still ample for washing, dishes and laundry.

⚠️ **Drain it completely before the first frost.** A frozen batch heater splits
the tank and the pipework.
⚠️ **Never seal it as a pressure vessel.** Always leave a vent or an open feed.
Heated water expands, and a sealed tank is a bomb.

# 7. Ollas and bucket drip irrigation

**1 h** · **€15** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Cuts garden watering by 50–70%**

## 7.1 Ollas — buried clay pots

Ancient, silent, and the most water-efficient irrigation there is. Water seeps
through the unglazed clay directly to the roots as the soil dries, with almost
zero evaporation.

**Build:** take two **unglazed terracotta pots**, plug the drainage hole of the
lower one with silicone or a cork, and glue the two rim-to-rim with silicone to
make a sealed vessel with a filling neck at the top. Or simply use a single large
unglazed pot with the hole plugged.

**Use:** bury to the neck, leaving 30 mm proud, between plants. **One 3–4 L olla
waters roughly 1 m²**. Fill weekly, cover with a stone or a saucer to stop
evaporation and mosquitoes. Lift them before winter or they crack.

## 7.2 Bucket drip line

**Build:** a 20 L bucket on a stand **500 mm above bed level**, with a tap at the
base feeding 16 mm poly tube laid along the bed, with 4 mm drippers or simply a
1 mm hole every 300 mm. Gravity does the rest.

**Details:** a fine mesh or a piece of cloth over the bucket outlet stops the
holes blocking · run it under mulch for maximum effect · **500 mm of head is
enough for a soaker line but not a sprinkler** · connect it to the IBC manifold
above for a garden-wide system. Feeding it with comfrey or nettle tea (strained!)
makes it a fertigation system too.

**The rule from Chapter 07: mulch saves more water than any irrigation system.**
Do the mulching first, then add drip for what mulch alone cannot cover.

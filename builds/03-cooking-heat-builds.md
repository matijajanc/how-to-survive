# 1. 16-brick rocket stove

**20 minutes** · **€15** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Outdoors only**

The highest fuel-efficiency-per-euro object in this entire folder. It boils
5 litres of water on **a double handful of twigs**, burns almost smokelessly, and
costs the price of sixteen bricks. Every household with any outdoor space should
have one.

```
            SECTION — the L-shaped rocket
                     ┌───┐
                     │ ▲ │  ← pot sits on the top, on 3 spacers,
                     │ ▲ │     leaving a gap for exhaust all round
        ┌────────────┤ ▲ ├─────┐
        │            │ ▲ │     │   RISER — the chimney.
        │            │ ▲ │     │   Height = 2–3 × the feed opening.
        │            │ ▲ │     │   This is the engine of the stove:
        │   ┌────────┘ ▲ └──┐  │   hot gas rising pulls air in hard,
        │   │  ═══════════  │  │   which is why it burns so cleanly.
      ══╪═══╡ FUEL SHELF    │  │
        │   │  ↑ air under  │  │ ← air MUST flow UNDER the sticks.
        └───┴───────────────┴──┘   Remove the shelf and it becomes a
              FEED                 smoky, useless fire.
```

**The three rules that make it work:**
1. **Equal cross-section throughout.** Feed opening, burn tunnel and riser should
   all be about the same area — roughly **90 × 90 mm** for a cooking stove.
   A pinch anywhere kills the draw.
2. **Riser height = 2–3 × the feed opening height.** The taller the riser, the
   harder it pulls.
3. **A fuel shelf**, so primary air flows *under* the burning sticks. This one
   detail is the difference between a rocket stove and a smoky hole.

**Materials:** 16–20 standard bricks (firebricks are better and last far longer;
ordinary bricks will spall and crack after a season or two of hard use) ·
3 small metal spacers or a cast-iron trivet · optionally wood ash, perlite or
vermiculite to insulate around the riser.

**Build**
1. Lay 4 bricks as a base pad on level ground.
2. Build an **L-shaped channel**: a horizontal feed tunnel about 2 bricks long,
   turning up into a vertical riser 3–4 bricks tall. Dry-stack, no mortar — so
   you can adjust it and take it apart.
3. Slide a **flat brick or a piece of steel plate into the feed tunnel as the fuel
   shelf**, leaving an air gap of 25–30 mm beneath it.
4. Pack **wood ash, perlite or vermiculite** into the gaps around the riser if
   you have a double-skin design. Insulation makes the riser hotter, which makes
   it burn cleaner and hotter still.
5. Put 3 spacers on top so the pot sits **20–25 mm above the riser**, letting
   exhaust escape all round. Sitting the pot flat on the riser smothers it.

**Use it:** feed small, dry sticks (finger-thick) in horizontally, pushing them
forward as they burn. Only the tips burn — this is what makes it so efficient.
**Use a windshield.** Expect to boil 5 L in 10–15 minutes on about 300 g of wood.

⚠️ Outdoors or in an open-sided shelter only. It produces CO like any fire.

# 2. Tin-can rocket stove

**45 min** · **€0–5** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Portable, outdoors only**

The same principle in scrap steel. Genuinely useful, and buildable from a
recycling bin.

**Materials:** one large can (4–5 L catering size) · one medium can (~1 L) for
the riser · one small can (soup size) for the feed · wood ash, perlite,
vermiculite or dry sand for insulation · tin snips · gloves.

**Build**
1. Cut a hole in the side of the **large can**, near the bottom, the diameter of
   the **small can**.
2. Cut a matching hole in the side of the **medium can** (the riser), near its
   bottom.
3. Push the small can through the large can's hole into the medium can's hole,
   forming an **L**. Seal the joints with foil or tuck the cut tabs in.
4. Stand the riser upright inside the large can and **pack the void between them
   with ash, perlite or vermiculite.** Insulation is not optional — it is what
   makes it burn hot and clean.
5. Cut a small rectangle of steel as a **fuel shelf** for the feed tube.
6. Make a pot stand from three bent tabs or a wire trivet, giving the same
   20–25 mm exhaust gap.

Lasts 1–3 seasons before the steel burns through. Build two.

# 3. Haybox / thermal cooker

**1 h** · **€0–20** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Cuts cooking fuel by 50–80%**

**If you build one thing from this folder, consider this one.** Bring food to the
boil for 10–15 minutes, then seal it in heavy insulation. It keeps cooking on
stored heat for 3–6 hours with **zero further fuel**. Beans, grains, stews, soups,
stocks, porridge — everything that needs a long simmer, which in a storage-food
diet is nearly everything.

```
        ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
        │ ░░░░ insulated lid / cushion ░░░░ │ ← must press down onto the pot
        ├───────────────────────────────────┤
        │ ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │
        │ ░░░  ┌───────────────────┐   ░░░  │
        │ ░░░  │  POT — lid on,    │   ░░░  │   100 mm+ of insulation
        │ ░░░  │  FULL, straight   │   ░░░  │   on ALL SIDES and
        │ ░░░  │  from a hard boil │   ░░░  │   underneath
        │ ░░░  └───────────────────┘   ░░░  │
        │ ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │
        └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Build:** a wooden box, a cardboard box, a cool-box, or a laundry basket — at
least **100 mm larger than the pot on every side and underneath**. Pack the void
with straw, hay, wool, shredded paper, polystyrene, old duvets, blankets or
sleeping bags. Make a thick insulated cushion for the top that **presses down
onto the pot lid**. Line it with foil or an old emergency blanket for a few extra
percent.

**Use it**
1. Bring the food to a **hard rolling boil and hold it there 10–15 minutes** (20
   for dry beans, 5 for rice). The pot must be genuinely hot all through.
2. Lid on, transfer **immediately** — every minute on the counter is heat lost.
3. Pack it in, cushion on top, close.
4. **Do not open it.** Every peek costs an hour of cooking.
5. Leave 3–6 hours. Dry beans that need 2 hours of simmering need 15 minutes of
   boil plus 4 hours in the box.

**Why it matters:** it turns your cooking fuel store into 2–4× as much cooking, it
works with any stove, it cannot burn food, and it keeps food hot for serving.
Combined with a pressure cooker (which cuts the boil phase too) it is the biggest
fuel saving available to any household.

⚠️ **Food safety:** the food must go in boiling hot and stay above 60 °C. A
well-built box holds a full pot above 70 °C for 5–6 hours. A half-empty pot in a
thin box does not — **use a full pot** (thermal mass is the point) and don't
stretch it past 6 hours.

# 4. Solar box oven

**4 h** · **€25** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Free cooking, April–September**

Reaches **120–150 °C** on a clear Central European summer day — enough to bake
bread, cook rice, stew vegetables, and pasteurise water. No fuel, no smoke, no
CO, no noise, and it cannot burn the house down.

```
              reflector lid (foil-covered, propped to bounce
             ╱  extra sun in — roughly doubles the temperature)
            ╱
         ┌─╱──────────────────────────┐
         │╱  GLASS or twin-wall lid   │ ← sealed, this is the greenhouse
         ├────────────────────────────┤
         │ ░░░ 50 mm insulation ░░░░  │
         │ ░ ┌──────────────────┐ ░   │
         │ ░ │ ▓▓ BLACK ▓▓▓▓▓▓  │ ░   │ ← black interior + a black, lidded
         │ ░ │ ▓▓ interior ▓▓▓  │ ░   │   cast-iron or dark enamel pot
         │ ░ └──────────────────┘ ░   │
         │ ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │
         └────────────────────────────┘
```

**Materials:** two cardboard boxes (one 50 mm smaller all round) or a plywood
box · 50 mm of insulation (crumpled newspaper, wool, straw, foam board) ·
aluminium foil · black matt paint or black card · a sheet of glass, an old
double-glazed unit, or twin-wall polycarbonate · black cooking pots with lids ·
a thermometer.

**Build**
1. Nest the small box inside the large one, packing insulation between them.
2. Line the **inside of the inner box matt black** (paint or black card) — black
   absorbs, shiny reflects. The *interior* must be black; only the *reflector*
   is shiny.
3. Make a well-fitting glazed lid. **Seal it** — the trapped air is what makes it
   work. A double-glazed unit is far better than a single sheet.
4. Add a hinged **reflector flap** lined with foil, propped at about 30° from the
   glass, to bounce extra sunlight in. This roughly doubles the temperature rise.
5. Put the thermometer inside where you can read it through the glass.

**Use it:** start 30–60 minutes before you need to cook (it needs to warm up) ·
re-aim it at the sun **every hour** · use dark, lidded pots · cooking takes
**roughly twice as long** as on a stove, and food cannot burn or dry out, so
timing is forgiving. It works from roughly March to October in Central Europe and
not at all in winter — so it is a summer fuel-saver and a preserving-season
workhorse, not a year-round solution.

# 5. Solar food dehydrator

**1 day** · **€50** · Skill ⬤⬤○ · **Preserve a glut with no jars and no power**

An **indirect** dehydrator — the sun heats air in a collector, and that hot air
flows up through the food. Better than drying in direct sun: no UV damage, better
colour and vitamin retention, and insects are kept out.

```
                              ┌────┐ ← chimney vent (hot air out;
                              │ ↑↑ │    this is what drives the airflow)
        ┌──────────────────┐  │    │
        │ ▤▤▤ TRAY 4 ▤▤▤▤▤ │  │    │
        │ ▤▤▤ TRAY 3 ▤▤▤▤▤ │  │    │   DRYING CHAMBER
        │ ▤▤▤ TRAY 2 ▤▤▤▤▤ │  │    │   mesh trays, 100 mm apart
        │ ▤▤▤ TRAY 1 ▤▤▤▤▤ │  │    │   shaded, insect-screened
        └────────▲─────────┘  └────┘
                 │ warm air rises
        ┌────────┴─────────────────┐
       ╱  glazed COLLECTOR, black   ╲  ← 1 m² of glazed black-painted
      ╱   inside, 30–45° to the sun  ╲    box; cool air enters at the
     ╱  ←←← cool air in (screened)    ╲   bottom, heats, and rises
    └──────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Materials:** plywood or salvaged timber · glass or twin-wall polycarbonate for
the collector · matt black paint · food-safe mesh (stainless, nylon or
food-grade plastic — **not galvanised**, zinc migrates into acidic food) for the
trays · insect screen · a small chimney.

**Build**
1. **Collector:** a shallow box about 1 m × 0.6 m, painted matt black inside,
   glazed on top, angled 30–45° south. Air inlet at the low end, screened.
2. **Chamber:** an insulated, *shaded* box above the collector's high end, with
   runners for 4–6 mesh trays at 100 mm spacing, and a door.
3. **Chimney:** a vent at the top of the chamber creates the stack effect that
   pulls air through. Screen it.
4. Everything screened against flies and wasps, which will otherwise find it
   within an hour.

**Use it:** slice thinly and evenly (3–6 mm) · a single layer per tray, not
touching · **rotate the trays** through the day since the bottom dries fastest ·
bring it all indoors at night, or moisture re-absorbs · **dry until
cracker-crisp** — anything leathery will mould in storage. Then condition it in a
jar for a week, shaking daily, and watch for condensation before packing for the
long term.

**In a Central European summer:** fruit and tomatoes take 1–3 days, herbs a few
hours, mushrooms a day. An electric dehydrator (300–500 W) run off surplus midday
solar is the weather-independent complement.

# 6. Wood store

**1 day** · **€80** · Skill ⬤⬤○ · **Holds ~4 m³ — one warm-room winter**

Seasoning is the whole point. Fresh wood is 45–60% water, gives **half the heat**,
and coats your chimney in creosote. A proper store gets wood to **under 20%**.

```
              ← roof slopes back, 200 mm overhang all round
        ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔╲
       │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │
       │  ░░░ THIS YEAR'S ░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │  3000 wide
       │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │  × 1000 deep
       │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │  × 1800 high
       │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │
       ═╪═══════════════════════════════╪═ ← bearers: wood 150 mm OFF the ground
        │                               │
      OPEN SIDES — wind must blow through. A closed store is a mould farm.
```

**Materials:** 6 × 70 × 70 posts (2400 mm) · 45 × 95 rails and bearers ·
corrugated roof sheet · 45 × 45 battens · post shoes or gravel · screws.

**Build**
1. Posts at 1.5 m centres along a **3 m × 1 m** footprint, in post shoes or on
   gravel — **never concreted straight into soil**, they rot in 5 years.
2. Rails front and back, roof slope 10° to the back.
3. **Bearers 150 mm above the ground**, running front-to-back so air passes under
   the stack. Pallets work perfectly.
4. Roof with **200 mm of overhang** on every side. Corrugated sheet on battens.
5. **Leave the sides completely open.** Cover the top only. Airflow through the
   stack is what dries it; wrapping a stack in tarpaulin traps moisture and grows
   fungus.
6. Site it in the **sun and the prevailing wind**, not tucked against a shaded
   north wall.

**Stack it properly:** split before stacking (split wood dries 4× faster than
round) · bark side up in the open, bark side down under cover · cross-stack the
ends for stability · leave finger gaps between rows.

**Run two stores**, or one store divided: **this winter's seasoned wood and next
winter's drying wood.** Hardwood needs 18–24 months, softwood 9–12. Buy a €15
moisture meter and actually measure a freshly split face — **under 20% or it
does not go in the stove.**

# 7. Warm-room tent

**1 h** · **€0–120** · Skill ⬤○○ · **🏢 the apartment heating answer**

When you cannot heat a room, heat a **volume**. An ordinary 3–4 person dome tent
pitched inside a living room, or a canopy over a bed, traps body heat in a small
space. It sounds absurd and it works extremely well: **a tent with two people in
it sits 10–15 °C above the room**, with no fuel at all.

**Options, cheapest first:**
1. **A four-poster bed canopy:** heavy blankets or a duvet draped over a frame of
   canes or a clothes airer around the bed. Free.
2. **An inner tent pitched in the room.** €60–120 for a used dome tent. Add a
   groundsheet, a foam camping mat and a rug underneath — **the floor is where
   most of the heat goes.**
3. **A room-in-a-room:** curtains or blankets hung to enclose the smallest corner
   of the room, ideally one with an internal wall on two sides.

**Details:**
- **Insulate underneath.** A mattress or two camping mats on the floor matters
  more than the walls.
- **Vent it.** A sealed tent with two people in it condenses heavily. Leave the
  top vent open — you will lose a little heat and gain dry bedding.
- **Hot water bottles** at the foot of each bed, refilled from the pot on the
  stove. A body in a good sleeping bag with a hot water bottle needs **no room
  heating at all**, down to well below freezing.
- ⚠️ **Never put any flame, catalytic heater or stove inside a tent or canopy.**
  Ever. This is the single most reliable way to die of carbon monoxide.
- Keep the rest of the flat **above 5 °C** to protect the plumbing, and drain
  the pipes if you cannot.

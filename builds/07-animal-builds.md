# 1. The chicken coop — full plan

**For 6–8 hens** · **2–3 days** · **€250–450 new, €80–150 from salvage** ·
Skill ⬤⬤○

## 1.1 The design rules that actually matter

Everything else is preference. Get these wrong and the coop fails.

| Rule | Number | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Coop floor area** | **0.4 m²/bird minimum** | Less and you get feather-pecking, bullying and disease |
| **Run area** | **2 m²/bird absolute minimum, 10 m² is much better** | A bare overgrazed run is a parasite and mud problem |
| **Perch length** | **200 mm/bird** | They all roost at night; short perches cause fights |
| **Perch shape** | **45 × 45 mm with rounded top edges — NOT a round dowel** | Chickens roost flat-footed and must cover their toes with their body in winter, or they get frostbite |
| **Nest boxes** | **1 per 4 hens**, 300 × 300 × 300 mm | More and they sleep in them (and foul them) |
| **Ventilation area** | **~0.1 m² per 1 m² of floor** → **0.24 m² for this coop** | **Damp, not cold, is what kills chickens.** Ammonia and moisture cause respiratory disease |
| **Vent position** | **Above the birds' heads, always** | Ventilation at perch height is a draught, and a draught is what makes them ill |
| **Mesh** | **12.5 mm (½") galvanised weld mesh, 19 gauge** | ❌ **A fox tears through chicken wire and a marten walks through it** |
| **Raised off the ground** | **500 mm** | Keeps the floor dry, denies rats a nest, gives the birds shade and shelter underneath |
| **Pop door** | **300 × 400 mm** | |

## 1.5 Build sequence

**Step 1 — Floor frame and legs (2 h)**
1. Screw the 45 × 95 perimeter together: 2000 rails outside, 1110 end rails
   between → external 2000 × 1200. **Check the diagonals are equal** before the
   last screw. A square floor makes everything after it easy.
2. Fit the three 1110 joists at ~490 mm centres.
3. Bolt a 600 mm leg into each **inside** corner with 2 × M8 coach bolts, top of
   leg flush with the top of the frame. 95 mm of overlap, two bolts, no wobble.
4. Screw the lower 45 × 45 braces between the legs ~150 mm above ground. This
   triangulates the base — **do not skip it**, an un-braced coop racks and
   eventually leans.
5. Stand it on paving slabs or gravel, not soil. Check it is level.
6. Lay the 18 mm ply floor, screwed at 200 mm centres. Seal the edges with paint
   or oil before you go further — the floor edge is what rots first.

**Step 2 — Wall frames (4 h)**
7. Build each wall flat on the ground, as a picture frame with studs between top
   and bottom plates. Screw through the plates into the stud ends (2 screws each).
8. Cut the side-wall top plates to the 14° slope. **Trick:** build both side
   frames as rectangles 1150 high, stand them up, mark the slope in place with a
   straight edge from the front wall to the back wall, then cut. Far easier than
   trigonometry.
9. Frame the openings: access door 900 × 800 with the sill 150 mm above the floor
   (stops bedding spilling out), pop door 300 × 400, sill 150 mm up.
10. Stand and screw the walls to the floor frame and to each other. Check square
    and plumb at every corner.

**Step 3 — Cladding and vents (3 h)**
11. Clad the outside with 12 mm OSB or boards. Leave the vent openings:
    - **A continuous 2000 × 80 mm strip along the top of the front wall**, under
      the roof overhang (0.16 m²)
    - **Two 200 × 150 openings high on the back wall** (0.06 m²)
    - **The triangles at the top of each side wall** (~0.06 m²)
    - **Total ≈ 0.28 m² — all of it above perch height.** This is the single most
      important detail in the whole build.
12. **Staple 12.5 mm weld mesh over every opening, on the inside**, then screw a
    batten over the edge. Staples alone pull out; a marten only needs one loose
    corner.
13. Overlap or seal all cladding joints. Water getting into the wall is what ends
    a coop's life.

**Step 4 — Roof (2 h)**
14. Fix the 5 rafters across the walls at 500 mm centres, with 250 mm overhanging
    the front and 150 mm the back. Notch them over the wall plates, or use angle
    brackets.
15. Deck with 12 mm OSB, then the corrugated sheet, fixing **through the crest of
    the corrugation** (never the trough) with washered nails or screws.
16. **Or, cheaper:** skip the deck and fix the corrugated sheet straight onto
    45 × 45 purlins at 450 mm centres.
17. Overhangs matter: they keep rain out of the vents and off the walls. Don't
    trim them back to save material.

**Step 5 — Doors, nest boxes, internals (3 h)**
18. **Access door:** 12 mm ply with a 45 × 45 frame behind it, hung on 2 hinges,
    closed with a hasp and a **two-step latch or a carabiner**. Foxes and martens
    solve simple turn-buttons and slide bolts. This is not a myth — use a fastener
    that needs two separate actions.
19. **Pop door:** a ply panel running in two vertical battens as guides, lifted by
    a cord over a small pulley on the roof edge to a cleat outside the run — so
    you can let them out without going in.
20. **Ramp:** 22 × 200 board, 1000 mm, with 20 × 20 cleats screwed every 100 mm.
21. **Nest boxes:** build a 650 × 350 × 400 box, cut two 300 × 300 openings
    through the back wall, hang the box outside with a **sloped, hinged lid**
    (slope it at 30–40° so they cannot roost on top of it and foul it). Put a
    100 mm lip on the front of each compartment to hold the bedding in. Nest floor
    300 mm above the coop floor.
22. **Perches:** 45 × 45, **round the top two edges with a plane or sandpaper**,
    sitting loose in notched brackets at 450 mm above the floor, 400 mm apart,
    300 mm from any wall. Removable = 10-second cleaning.
23. **Droppings board:** 12 mm ply on battens, 250 mm below the perches. Slides
    out. **Catches ~60% of all the manure the birds produce** — scrape it into a
    bucket each morning and the coop stays clean with almost no effort. The single
    best quality-of-life feature in the design.

**Step 6 — Finish (1 h)**
24. Two or three coats of raw linseed oil or exterior paint. **Inside as well as
    out** — the inside is what gets damp.
25. Deep litter: 100 mm of wood shavings or chopped straw on the floor.
26. Leave it a few days to air before the birds move in.

## 1.6 What ruins a coop

```
❌ Chicken wire instead of weld mesh          → you will lose the flock
❌ Not enough ventilation                     → respiratory disease all winter
❌ Ventilation at perch height                → draughts, sick birds
❌ Round perches / sharp-edged perches        → frostbitten toes, bumblefoot
❌ No droppings board                         → you stop cleaning it, then disease
❌ Sitting on the ground                      → rot, rats nesting underneath
❌ Simple slide bolts                         → martens and foxes open them
❌ Nest boxes at or above perch height        → they sleep in them and foul the eggs
❌ Too small "because they're outside all day" → winter, rain and broodiness say otherwise
❌ Flat roof / no overhang                    → leaks, wet walls, wet vents
❌ No access for cleaning                     → you won't clean it
```

# 2. The predator-proof run

**12 m² for 6 birds** · **1 day** · **€230–280** · Skill ⬤⬤○

```
              PLAN — 3000 × 4000 mm                    SECTION at the fence
    ┌────────────────────────────────────┐          ║ 25 mm mesh or netting
    │  ○        ○         ○         ○    │          ║   (600 mm)
    │                                    │          ╠═══ 1200 mm
    │            COOP                    │          ║ 12.5 mm WELD MESH
    │          (in a corner,             │          ║   (predator band)
    │           or outside               │          ║
    │           with a pop-             │  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓╩▓▓▓  ← ground level
    │           door tunnel)             │  ▓ 450 mm APRON, mesh laid FLAT
    │                                    │  ▓ on the ground, pegged, turfed over
    │  ○        ○      GATE   ○     ○    │
    └────────────────────────┬───────────┘   A fox digs AT the fence line, hits
       ○ = 70 × 70 post at 2 m centres       the apron, and gives up. Far easier
                                             and more effective than burying.
```

**Materials:** 8 × 70 × 70 posts, 2400 mm (or 75 × 75 fence posts) · 14 m of
45 × 45 top rail · **1 roll of 12.5 mm weld mesh, 1.2 m × 25 m** · 14 m × 0.6 m
of 25 mm mesh or netting for the upper band · 12 m² of bird netting for the top ·
galvanised staples · 40 ground pegs · gate hardware.

**Build:**
1. Set the posts at 2 m centres, 600 mm into the ground on gravel (or in metal
   post shoes — they last far longer). 1800 mm above ground so you can walk in.
2. Top rail all round, at post height.
3. **Weld mesh 1200 mm high** stapled to the posts every 100 mm, tight.
4. **The apron:** lay 450 mm of mesh flat on the ground, running *outward* from
   the base of the fence, all the way round. Peg it every 300 mm, then cover with
   soil or turf. Grass grows through it and it disappears in a season.
5. Upper band of cheaper 25 mm mesh from 1200 to 1800 mm.
6. **Net or mesh the top.** Hawks take chickens in broad daylight, and hens fly
   better than people expect.
7. Gate 900 mm wide, with the same two-step latch as the coop.
8. **Rotate if you can:** two runs used alternately, six months each, stops the
   ground becoming a bare parasite-laden mud pit. Sow the resting one with grass,
   clover and fodder kale.

⚠️ **Check the whole perimeter monthly** and after every storm. One lifted staple
is a whole flock.

# 3. Chicken tractor (mobile)

**For 3–5 birds** · **1 day** · **€150** · Skill ⬤⬤○

A bottomless A-frame coop-and-run on wheels. Moved a metre or two each day, it
gives the birds fresh grass and insects continuously, spreads their manure evenly,
and needs no run at all. **The best way to cut the feed bill**, and excellent for
clearing and fertilising a bed before planting.

```
           ╱╲              2000 mm long × 1200 wide × 1200 high
          ╱  ╲             45 × 45 A-frames at 0 / 1000 / 2000 mm
         ╱ ▓▓ ╲            One third enclosed and clad (shelter, perch, nest)
        ╱ ▓▓▓▓ ╲           Two thirds open frame with 12.5 mm weld mesh
       ╱________╲          Handles one end, two wheels the other
      ═╧════════╧═         Weight target: under 25 kg so one person can move it
```

**Key points:** keep it light (45 × 45 and thin cladding only) · a skirt of mesh
laid flat around the base at night, or move it inside a fence · **no floor** —
that is the point · a nest box you can reach from outside · move it *every day*
or the grass under it dies.

# 4. No-waste feeder and drinkers

## 4.1 Port feeder — 30 min, €20

A 20 L bucket with **90° PVC elbows (75 mm)** pushed through holes cut 80 mm up
from the base. The bird puts its head into the elbow to feed; nothing gets
scratched out onto the floor.

1. Mark 3–4 holes around the bucket, 80 mm from the bottom.
2. Cut with a hole saw to a tight fit on the elbow.
3. Push the elbows in, opening pointing **down and out**, seal with silicone.
4. Fill, fit the lid, hang or stand it at **bird-back height** (about 300 mm) —
   feeding at back height stops them scratching feed out and keeps rats off.

**Saves 20–30% of your feed bill**, which over a year is 60–90 kg of grain. It
also keeps feed dry and much less attractive to rodents. Build this one first.

## 4.2 Nipple drinker — 30 min, €20

A 20 L bucket with **3–4 horizontal poultry nipples** screwed into the base
(8 mm drill, they self-tap). Clean water, no droppings, no spillage, no daily
scrubbing. Hang at head height so the birds reach slightly up.

## 4.3 Keeping water liquid in winter — the job nobody plans for

**A frozen drinker is the most common winter chicken killer.** Options, in order:
- **Two drinkers, swapped** morning and evening from the house. Free, reliable,
  and it always works.
- **Black rubber tub in the sun** — thaws itself on sunny days, and you can flex
  the ice out rather than chipping it.
- **Insulated box** around the bucket (polystyrene in a plywood case), lid on top.
- **Solar-powered: a 5–15 W aquarium or pipe heater on a thermostat**, run from
  the 12 V system (see [Power builds](02-power-builds.md)). 15 W for 8 hours is
  120 Wh/day — well within a modest system, and it removes the job entirely.

# 5. Rabbit hutch — 4 compartment

**1 buck + 3 does** · **1 day** · **€120–180** · Skill ⬤⬤○

```
                2400 mm wide × 700 deep × 2 tiers
    ┌───────────────────┬───────────────────┐
    │  DOE 1            │  DOE 2            │   Each compartment:
    │  1200 × 700 × 650 │                   │   1200 W × 700 D × 650 H
    ├───────────────────┼───────────────────┤
    │  DOE 3            │  BUCK             │   Nest box space at one end
    │                   │                   │   Wire floor over a slide-out tray
    └───────────────────┴───────────────────┘
      ║               ║               ║         Raised 700 mm on legs
      ║               ║               ║         Sloping roof, 300 mm overhang
    ══╩═══════════════╩═══════════════╩══       all round (sun and rain)
```

**Materials:** 45 × 45 frame · 12 mm OSB or ply for the backs, ends and dividers ·
**12.5 mm weld mesh** for the doors and fronts · **galvanised weld mesh 12 × 12
or 16 × 16 mm for the floors** · corrugated roof sheet · galvanised trays or
sheet metal for the droppings · hinges and latches.

**The details that matter:**
- **Shade is more important than warmth.** Rabbits handle −15 °C easily but die
  above 28–30 °C. Site the hutch in shade, with a deep roof overhang, and give it
  cross-ventilation in summer. **Overheating is the number one killer of hutch
  rabbits.**
- **Wire floors** (16 × 16 or 12 × 25 mm) let droppings fall through to a tray —
  far cleaner and healthier than solid floors. But **always give a solid resting
  board** (a plain piece of ply, 300 × 300) in each compartment, or they get
  sore hocks.
- **Nest box** for each doe: 400 × 300 × 300 with a 150 mm lip, added 3 days
  before kindling and removed at weaning.
- **Hay rack** on the front of each compartment — hay is their staple and it must
  be available constantly and kept off the floor.
- **Raise it 700 mm** — comfortable to work at, dry, and no rats underneath.
- **Rabbit manure is "cold"** — it goes straight onto the garden with no
  composting. Put the trays where you can slide them out and barrow them to a bed.

# 6. Quail cage — stackable

**10 birds per tier** · **4 h** · **€60** · Skill ⬤⬤○ · **🏢 works on a balcony**

```
            1000 mm wide × 600 deep × 250 high per tier
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  mesh top (soft: netting or plastic)    │ ← 250 mm ceiling ONLY
    │                                          │   ⚠️ quail startle and fly
    │   10 birds                     ╲         │   straight up — a hard roof
    │                                 ╲ 7°     │   at 400 mm breaks necks
    ├──────────────────────────────────╲──────┤
    │  droppings tray (slide out)       └─egg─┘ ← floor sloped 7° so eggs
    └─────────────────────────────────────────┘    roll to a front gutter
      feeder and drinker on the OUTSIDE, birds feed through the front mesh
```

**Build:** a 45 × 45 or 20 × 45 frame, 12.5 mm weld mesh on the sides, **16 × 16
or 12 × 12 mm mesh floor sloped 7°** to a front lip that catches the eggs, a
slide-out tray underneath (a baking tray or sheet metal), and a **soft top** —
netting or plastic mesh, not plywood.

**Details:**
- **0.1 m²/bird.** 10 birds in 1000 × 600 is right; crowding causes feather
  pecking, which in quail escalates fast.
- **250 mm ceiling height, soft top.** Quail flush vertically when startled.
  A low soft ceiling is a welfare requirement, not a space-saving trick.
- **Feeder and drinker outside the cage**, accessed through the mesh — keeps feed
  clean and makes daily care a 20-second job.
- **Stack 2–3 tiers** with the trays between them. 3 tiers = 30 birds = ~25 eggs
  a day in 0.6 m² of floor space.
- Litter the tray with sawdust or paper and change it **every 2–3 days** — quail
  produce a lot for their size and ammonia builds fast in a flat.
- ⚠️ **They do not go broody.** A small incubator (€60, ~40 W for 17 days) is
  what makes this a renewable cycle. Build it into your power plan.

# 7. Black soldier fly larvae bucket

**30 min** · **€15** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Free protein from kitchen waste**

BSF larvae convert food waste into 40%-protein feed, and **they harvest
themselves** — when mature they crawl up a ramp looking for somewhere dry to
pupate, and fall into a jar.

```
        ┌─────────────────┐  vented lid (mesh)
        │  food waste     │
        │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░   │        ╱── 35–40° ramp (a strip of ply or
        │  ░ larvae ░░    │      ╱      corrugated plastic) up to a hole
        │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░  ╱│    ╱
        │              ╱  │  ╱  ┌────┐
        └────────────╱────┘─────│ jar│ ← self-harvested larvae collect here
         20 L bucket            └────┘
         drainage holes + gravel in the base
```

**Build:** drill 6 mm drainage holes in the base of a 20 L bucket and add 50 mm
of gravel · fit a ramp at **35–40°** from the base to a 40 mm hole near the rim ·
a pipe or chute from the hole to a collecting jar · mesh-vented lid · keep it in
shade, 25–30 °C.

**Feed it:** fruit and vegetable waste, spoiled produce, bread, brewing waste,
manure. **Not** meat or dairy in a domestic setting (smell and rats).
**Yield:** roughly 1 kg of larvae per 10 kg of waste — straight to chickens,
quail or fish.
⚠️ BSF need warmth; in Central Europe they work April–October outdoors, or
year-round in a warm shed.

# 8. Fodder sprouting rack

**2 h** · **€40** · Skill ⬤○○ · **Fresh green feed in winter**

1 kg of barley becomes **6–8 kg of green fodder mat in 7 days**. Be clear about
what this does: it does **not** create calories (dry matter falls slightly) — it
converts stored dry grain into fresh green feed with vitamins, which is exactly
what confined animals lack from November to March.

**Build:** a shelf unit with **7 trays** (one per day of the cycle), each
tray drilled with 3 mm drainage holes and **tilted 10°** to a collection gutter,
the whole rack draining into a bucket.

**Run it:**
```
Day 0  Soak 1 kg of barley 12 h → drain → spread 10–15 mm deep in tray 1
Day 1–6  Rinse every tray twice a day; they drain into each other and the bucket
Day 7  Tray 1 is a 6–8 kg root-bound green mat. Cut it out, feed it whole,
       re-sow the tray. One tray harvested every single day, forever.
```
Keep it at 18–21 °C, out of direct sun, with good airflow. **Mould is the enemy** —
rinse properly, don't sow too thick, and throw out any tray that smells sour.

# 9. Rodent-proof feed store

**1 h** · **€40–80** · Skill ⬤○○

**Rats and mice will eat, foul and destroy more of your feed than your animals
do**, and they carry disease into the coop. A sack of feed on a shed floor is a
rodent feeding station.

**Options, best first:**
1. **Galvanised steel dustbins with tight lids** (€30 each, ~60 kg of feed each).
   Rodents cannot chew galvanised steel. Bungee the lid down against foxes.
2. **A plywood box lined completely with 6 mm hardware cloth** — floor, walls and
   lid. More work, holds more, and looks like furniture.
3. **IBC tote** with a screw lid, for bulk grain.
4. ❌ **Not** plastic bins, not sacks, not "up on a shelf". Rats climb, and they
   chew through 5 mm polypropylene in an evening.

**Also:** keep the store **away from the coop** (don't concentrate the attraction
where the birds sleep), sweep spills immediately, and keep 12 snap traps set
around the perimeter as standard practice, not as a response to a problem.

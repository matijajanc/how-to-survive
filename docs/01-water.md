# 01 — Water

*Three days without it and you are dead. One week of drinking bad water and you
are dead more slowly and much worse. This is the chapter to finish first.*

---

## 1. How much you actually need

Forget "2 litres a day". That is drinking water for a sedentary person in mild
weather. A household doing manual work, cooking from raw ingredients, washing by
hand and keeping a garden alive needs far more.

| Use | Litres/person/day | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking | 2.5 – 3.5 | Up to 6 L with heavy work or fever/diarrhoea |
| Cooking & food prep | 2 – 3 | Dry beans and grains need soaking and boiling water |
| Washing hands & face | 2 – 3 | **Do not cut this.** It is your disease control. |
| Body washing | 3 – 5 | A sponge bath is 3 L; a shower is 40–80 L |
| Dish washing | 2 – 3 | Two-basin method: wash + rinse |
| Toilet flushing | 6 – 20 | Only if you use a flush toilet — see Ch. 05 |
| Laundry | 3 – 5 | Averaged over a week; ~30 L per hand-wash load |
| **Household total** | **15 – 25** | Realistic sustainable planning figure |
| **Survival minimum** | **5 – 7** | Drinking + cooking + minimal hygiene. Survivable for weeks, miserable, and hygiene risk rises. |

Add on top:
- **Animals:** chicken 0.25–0.5 L/day · rabbit 0.5–1 L · goat 4–8 L · dog 1–2 L ·
  pig 10–20 L
- **Garden:** in a dry Central European summer, roughly **3–5 L/m²/day** for
  intensively cropped beds, much less with heavy mulch and drip irrigation
  (1–2 L/m²/day). A 100 m² garden can want 300 L on a hot day. **This is why
  garden water must come from rain, not from your drinking store.**

### Your household number

```
Persons ___ × 20 L         = _____ L/day
Animals (sum)              = _____ L/day
Garden (summer only)       = _____ L/day
                             --------
                 TOTAL/day = _____ L/day
        × 14 (L1 target)   = _____ L
        × 90 (L2 target)   = _____ L
```

For a family of four: **80 L/day**, so **1,120 L for two weeks** and
**7,200 L for three months.** You are not going to store 7,200 L of drinking
water in a flat. This is exactly why the plan is *storage + a renewable source +
treatment*, not storage alone.

---

## 2. The three-layer water strategy

Every household needs all three layers. Storage alone is a countdown timer.

```
LAYER 1 — STORED        Immediate, clean, zero effort. Covers days 1–14.
LAYER 2 — SOURCE        Rain, well, spring, stream, lake. Covers months.
LAYER 3 — TREATMENT     Turns Layer 2 into Layer 1. The most important layer.
```

Treatment is the layer people skip and it is the one that decides whether you get
cholera. A household with a stream and no filter is worse off than a household
with 200 L and a Sawyer filter.

---

## 3. Layer 1 — Storage

### How much, and in what

**Target: 15 L/person/day × 14 days = 200 L per person minimum (L1).**
For L2, either 600 L/person stored, or 200 L stored plus a proven source.

| Container | Capacity | €/unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reused 5 L / 10 L PET bottles | 5–10 L | Free | Perfect starter. Washed, filled, dated. Stack in a cool dark place. Replace annually. |
| Food-grade jerry can | 20 L | €15–25 | The workhorse. Portable when full (20 kg). Buy 6–10. |
| Blue food-grade barrel | 60 / 120 / 200 L | €40–90 | Cheap per litre. **A full 200 L drum weighs 200 kg — you will never move it.** Site it before filling, on a solid floor, with a siphon pump (€10). |
| IBC tote (caged tank) | 1,000 L | €80–180 used | Best €/litre by far. Must be food-grade and previously held only food (verify!). Opaque cover or paint — sunlight grows algae. Needs a firm level base; 1,000 kg. |
| Bathtub bladder (WaterBOB type) | 250–380 L | €25–40 | **Apartment essential.** Deploy at AMBER, before pressure fails. Bathtubs themselves leak and are filthy; the liner solves both. |
| Stainless/plastic water butt | 200–1,000 L | €60–200 | For rainwater, outdoors. |

**Rules for stored water**
- Food-grade containers only (HDPE marked with a "2" or "food safe"). Never reuse
  containers that held chemicals, fuel or milk (milk protein residue is impossible
  to fully remove and will spoil).
- Fill with treated mains water if you have it. If your mains is chlorinated, you
  can store it as-is.
- To store longer than ~6 months, add **2 drops of unscented 5% sodium
  hypochlorite bleach per litre** (≈ 2 mg/L free chlorine) at fill time, or use a
  NaDCC tablet at the dose on the packet.
- **Date every container.** Rotate mains-filled storage every 6–12 months — the
  water doesn't "go off" chemically but plastics leach and biofilm grows.
- Store cool, dark, off concrete floors (use pallets or boards — concrete leaches
  and sweats), away from petrol, paint and solvents (plastics are permeable to
  vapours).
- **Freezing splits containers.** Leave 10% headspace and keep storage above 0 °C,
  or store in a place you accept losing. Unheated garages in Central Europe will
  freeze.

### The hidden water already in your home (know this now)

At the moment pressure fails, you already have:

| Source | Typical volume | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot water cylinder / boiler tank** | **80–200 L** | The biggest single reserve in most homes. Turn off power/gas to it and close the cold inlet *first*, then open the drain valve at the bottom and a hot tap upstairs to let air in. Learn where these valves are **today**. |
| Toilet cisterns | 6–9 L each | Clean water (the tank, not the bowl). Usable directly. |
| Pipes in the house | 5–15 L | Open the lowest tap in the building and a high one to vent. |
| Ice cubes, drinks, tinned vegetable liquid | A few L | Counts in an emergency |
| Central heating system | 50–150 L | **Not potable** — contains inhibitor chemicals. Fine for flushing toilets. |
| Waterbed, aquarium, pool | Large | Not potable; flushing and washing only. Pool water can be filtered for washing but is chemically loaded. |

**First action at AMBER:** fill everything — bath, bladder, jerry cans, pots,
bottles, the washing machine drum, buckets. Water is free and heavy; the moment
you want it, it stops being available.

## 4. Layer 2 — Sources you control

### 🏡 Rainwater — the best option for anyone with a roof

**The maths (memorise this):**

```
Litres = Roof footprint (m²) × Rainfall (mm) × 0.8
```

The 0.8 is the runoff coefficient (losses to splash, evaporation, first-flush,
absorption). Use 0.9 for smooth metal, 0.75–0.8 for tile, 0.7 for rough or
mossy surfaces.

**So 1 mm of rain on a 100 m² roof ≈ 80 litres.**

A house with a 120 m² roof footprint in a climate with 1,000 mm/year
(typical inland Central Europe; Slovenia is often 1,000–1,500 mm) yields:

```
120 × 1000 × 0.8 = 96,000 L per year  ≈  260 L/day averaged
```

That is more than a family of four needs, from the roof alone. **Rain is the
answer for the land track, and the limiting factor is storage, not rainfall.**

**Sizing storage:** size for your longest dry spell, not your annual total. In
Central Europe, plan for a **3–5 week dry summer period**. Family of four at
80 L/day × 30 days = **2,400 L**, i.e. three IBC totes. Add more if you irrigate:
a garden in a hot July can use 3,000 L/month on its own. Realistic land-track
target: **3,000–6,000 L of rainwater storage** (3–6 IBCs, or one buried/above
ground tank).

**Build order for a rainwater system**
1. **Gutters and downpipes** — clean, sound, with leaf guards.
2. **First-flush diverter** — discards the first 0.5–1 mm of each rain event,
   which carries the bird droppings, dust and pollen. Cheap (€30–60) or DIY from
   a vertical pipe with a floating ball. **Do not skip this**; it does more for
   water quality than any filter downstream.
3. **Coarse screen/leaf filter** at the tank inlet (1 mm mesh) — keeps out
   debris, leaves and, critically, mosquitoes and rodents.
4. **Tank(s)**, opaque or shaded, with a sealed lid, an overflow routed away from
   the house foundations, and a drain at the bottom for sludge.
5. **Take-off above the floor** — draw water 10–15 cm above the tank bottom so
   you leave the sediment layer behind (calmed inlet + floating suction is even
   better).
6. **Link tanks** at the base with a manifold so they fill and draw as one.
7. **Treatment before drinking.** Always. See Layer 3.

**Roof material warnings:** Never drink rainwater from asbestos-cement, lead
flashing, tar/bitumen shingles (leaches hydrocarbons), copper with acidic rain,
or any recently treated/painted roof. Clay tile, concrete tile, slate, glazed
tile, and coated steel are fine. If in doubt, use roof water for the garden,
flushing and washing only, and drink from stored/other sources.

**Winter:** disconnect and drain downpipe filters and exposed pipework before the
first hard frost, or they split. Keep one tank indoors/insulated, or accept that
outdoor tanks are frozen solid in January — which is exactly when you need them.
Partially burying a tank, or building a straw-bale surround, keeps it liquid.

### 🏡 Wells and boreholes

If you have or can get a well, it is the single most valuable resilience asset a
property can have — *provided you can lift the water without electricity.*

- **Shallow/dug well (< 8 m to water):** a suction hand pump works. Cheap and simple.
- **Deep well/borehole (8–80 m):** needs a deep-well hand pump (piston pump with
  rods, e.g. Bison/Simple Pump types, €800–2,500) that can often be installed
  *alongside* the existing electric submersible pump, or a **DC solar pump**
  (€300–900 for a 12/24 V pump + panel). Both approaches are worth it.
- **Test the water** (bacteriology + nitrate + arsenic + heavy metals) before you
  rely on it. Nitrate from agricultural land is common and is not removed by
  boiling or ordinary filters — it needs reverse osmosis or ion exchange, and it
  is dangerous for infants.
- **Never** assume well water is clean because it is clear. Treat it, or test it
  annually.
- Keep a spare pump seal/leather kit, and know how to pull and service the pump.

### 🏡 Springs, streams, ponds, lakes

Free, renewable, and **always contaminated** — assume giardia, cryptosporidium,
*E. coli*, and agricultural/industrial runoff. Usable with full treatment.
- Collect from flowing water where possible; avoid stagnant water and anything
  downstream of fields, roads, septics or livestock.
- Pre-settle turbid water in a bucket for several hours, or filter through cloth,
  before the real filter — silt destroys filter elements fast.
- Note the distance and the effort: **20 L weighs 20 kg**. A 200 m round trip
  four times a day is a real daily job. Plan a cart, a yoke, or a gravity line.

### 🏡 Snow and ice
1 volume of snow yields roughly 0.1 of water. Melting snow costs a lot of fuel —
never eat snow (it lowers core temperature). Only a last resort.

### 🏢 Apartment sources
You control almost nothing, so storage and pre-emptive filling are everything:
- Bathtub bladder (250–380 L) — deploy at AMBER.
- 8–12 × 20 L jerry cans stored under beds and in cupboards (160–240 L).
- Your hot water cylinder (80–200 L) if you have one — know the drain valve.
- Balcony rain catch: even a 2 m² tarp or an awning funnelled into a butt gives
  **1.6 L per mm of rain** — 30–80 L in a decent month. Small but free, and ideal
  for flushing and washing, which is where most of your litres go.
- Building roof: if you can legally access it and your block has a flat roof, a
  single downpipe can supply the whole building. **This is a conversation to have
  with your building committee now, not during a crisis.** A shared 1,000 L IBC in
  a utility room, fed from a roof downpipe, is transformative for a block of flats.
- **Never rely on being able to carry water up 8 flights with no lift.** 20 L per
  trip; a family's daily need is 4 trips. Plan a rope-and-bucket haul from a
  balcony, and store more so you need fewer trips.

## 5. Layer 3 — Treatment: filtration and disinfection

**Understand the distinction, because most people get it wrong:**

- **Filtration** physically removes particles, protozoa (giardia, crypto) and —
  if fine enough (≤ 0.2 µm) — bacteria. Most filters **do not remove viruses.**
- **Disinfection** (chemical or heat) kills bacteria and viruses but does **not**
  remove sediment, chemicals, or (for chlorine) cryptosporidium reliably.

**Therefore: always do both, in this order.**

```
SETTLE → PRE-FILTER (cloth/sand) → FILTER (≤0.2 µm) → DISINFECT (chlorine/boil/UV) → STORE CLEAN
```

### Boiling — the gold standard
Bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute** (3 minutes above 2,000 m). That kills
everything biological. It does not remove chemicals, nitrate or sediment, and it
costs fuel: roughly **0.1 kWh per litre** to heat from 15 °C to boiling —
about 1 kg of firewood per 10–15 L, or 90 g of propane per 10 L. Boiling all your
water is fuel-expensive; use it for drinking/cooking water when filters fail, and
filter+chlorinate for the rest.

### Chemical disinfection — cheap, light, stores well

**Unscented liquid bleach (sodium hypochlorite, 5–6%, no additives/fragrance):**
- **2 drops per litre** of clear water (4 drops if cloudy or cold).
- ≈ **10 mL (2 tsp) per 40 L**, or ≈ **50 mL per 200 L drum**.
- Stir, wait **30 minutes** (60 min if cold or turbid). You should smell a faint
  chlorine odour afterwards — if not, repeat the dose and wait again.
- **Bleach degrades.** It loses ~20% potency per year at room temperature, faster
  when warm. Date your bottles, store cool and dark, and replace annually.
  This is why bleach is a poor *long-term* store.

**Calcium hypochlorite granules ("pool shock", 65–70% available chlorine)** —
the long-term answer. Stores for years dry and sealed, and **500 g treats
roughly 150,000–190,000 litres.**
- Make a stock solution: **7 g (≈1 heaped tsp) into 7.5 L of water** ≈ a 0.5–1%
  chlorine solution.
- Then dose **1 part stock solution to 100 parts water** to treat, and wait 30 min.
- **Safety:** buy only the plain calcium hypochlorite type with no algaecide,
  clarifier, stabiliser or anti-fungal additives. Store in a sealed glass or HDPE
  container away from anything flammable, acids, or ammonia — it is a strong
  oxidiser and reacts violently, including with organic matter and some plastics.
  Never store in a metal container. Keep it dry; it releases chlorine gas if damp.
  Store outside the house (shed/garage) if possible.

**NaDCC tablets (sodium dichloroisocyanurate — Aquatabs, Katadyn Micropur etc.)**
- The most convenient and safest-storing option. **5–10 year shelf life**, exact
  dosing, no handling hazard. Typically one tablet per 1 L, 5 L or 20 L depending
  on the product. ~€0.03 per litre treated.
- Buy a mix: a bulk tin of 20 L tablets for the household store, and a small
  bottle of 1 L tablets for the go-bag.
- **This should be in every household, both tracks, at every level.** Cheap, tiny,
  and it works.

**Iodine** — works but tastes vile, is less effective against cryptosporidium,
and must not be used long-term by pregnant people or anyone with thyroid disease.
Keep as a backup only.

### Filters — what to buy

| Type | Pore size | Throughput | Life | € | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gravity ceramic/carbon (Berkey-type, 2 × elements)** | ~0.2 µm | 4–8 L/h | 10,000–20,000 L per pair | €250–350 | **The household workhorse.** No power, no pumping, filters continuously on the worktop. Elements are cleanable (scrub with a scouring pad). Buy spares. |
| **Hollow-fibre squeeze/inline (Sawyer Squeeze / Mini)** | 0.1 µm | 1–2 L/min | up to 100,000+ L | €30–45 | Astonishing value. Buy 3–4. Bucket-mount them for a DIY gravity system for €50 total. **Must not freeze** — freezing ruptures the fibres invisibly and it will then pass everything. |

**Minimum recommended kit (both tracks):**
1 gravity filter (Berkey-type or bucket-mounted Sawyers) **+** 2 spare Sawyer
filters **+** a tin of NaDCC tablets **+** 500 g calcium hypochlorite (land) or a
1 L bleach bottle rotated annually (apartment) **+** a cooking pot you can boil in.
Total: €150–400. This covers you for years.

### Turning cloudy water clear
Filters clog fast on silty water. Pre-treat:
1. **Settle** — stand it in a bucket for 2–12 hours; decant the clear top.
2. **Cloth filter** — 4–8 layers of cotton (a folded T-shirt or a tea towel).
   Remarkably effective; this alone removes a large share of pathogens attached
   to particles.
3. **Coagulate** — a pinch of alum (aluminium sulphate) or even crushed
   *Moringa* seed makes particles clump and settle in 30 minutes. Alum is cheap,
   stores forever, and is worth having.
4. **Sand/charcoal pre-filter** — gravel, coarse sand, fine sand, charcoal in a
   bucket. Improves clarity and taste. **It is a pre-filter, not a purifier.**

### Storing treated water
Once treated, store in clean, closed, dark containers and **use within 2–3 days**
unless you added a chlorine residual. Never dip dirty cups into a store — use a
tap or a dedicated pouring vessel. Recontamination at the point of use is one of
the most common causes of illness in a crisis.

## 6. Using less — the highest-leverage move

Cutting demand is cheaper than any tank or filter.

- **Two-basin dishwashing:** wash basin + rinse basin. 4 L does a family's meal
  instead of 20 L of running water. Reuse the rinse water as the next wash water.
- **Sponge bath / bucket bath:** 3–5 L versus 60 L for a shower. A **solar shower
  bag** (20 L, €15) or a garden-sprayer shower (€20, pressurised, 5 L does two
  people) makes it civilised.
- **Navy shower:** wet, water off, soap, water on, rinse. 10–15 L.
- **Don't flush drinking water.** A toilet flush is 6–9 L of potable water.
  Switch to greywater flushing or a bucket toilet (Ch. 05) on day one. For a
  family of four this alone saves **50–80 L/day** — the single biggest water
  saving available in any household.
- **Cook in ways that keep the water:** steam over a pot and reuse the water;
  cook pasta in less water; keep vegetable cooking water for soup or the garden.
- **Catch the warm-up water.** A bucket under the tap while hot water arrives
  saves 1–3 L per use, several times a day.

## 7. Greywater — reusing what you already used

Greywater = washing, bathing, laundry, and dish water (not toilet = blackwater).
In a shortage this is free water for flushing and the garden.

- **Use it within 24 hours.** It turns anaerobic and foul fast.
- **Use plant-safe soap** if it goes on the garden: no boron, no chlorine bleach,
  low sodium. Simple soap and potash-based soaps are fine; most laundry powders
  are not (high sodium and phosphate build up in soil).
- **Apply to soil, never to foliage, and never to root crops or anything eaten
  raw.** Fruit trees, berry bushes, sweetcorn, potatoes, and ornamentals are ideal.
- Do not store it, do not let it pond, and keep it away from the water source and
  from where children play.
- The simplest system: a basin in the sink → carry to a bucket → flush the toilet
  or water the fruit trees. No plumbing needed, and it's legal everywhere.

## 8. Water safety — the non-negotiables

- **Assume every untreated source is contaminated.** Clear, cold and fast-flowing
  means nothing.
- **Separate clean and dirty containers absolutely.** Colour-code them. Mark
  "RAW" and "DRINK" in permanent marker. Never let them touch or swap roles.
- **Wash hands before handling drinking water.** Recontamination at the tap is
  the classic failure.
- **Diarrhoea is the emergency.** It kills by dehydration, fast, especially in
  children and elderly. Have **oral rehydration salts** in stock, and know the
  home recipe: **1 L clean water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + ½ level teaspoon
  salt.** Sip continuously. This saves lives — it is the single highest-value
  item in your medical kit (see Ch. 06).
- **Don't drink from the heating system, the pool, or anything with antifreeze,
  inhibitors or algaecide.** Ever.

```
[ ] Household daily requirement calculated and written down
[ ] L1 storage in place (200 L/person) and dated

```

# 09 — The Apartment Playbook 🏢

*Everything in chapters 01–06 applies to you. This chapter covers what is
different, what is harder, and — the part that surprises people — what a flat can
actually produce.*

---

## 1. The honest limits, stated up front

**You cannot feed yourself from a flat.** Not from a balcony, not with grow
lights, not with a vertical garden. A 6 m² balcony in full sun, worked
expertly, might produce 15–30 kg of vegetables a season — perhaps **3–5 days'
worth of calories over a whole year**. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling
something.

**So the apartment strategy is different in kind, not just in scale:**

```
🏡 LAND:      store food  +  PRODUCE food        → aims at self-sufficiency
🏢 APARTMENT: store food  +  PRODUCE NUTRITION   → aims at endurance
```

Your stored calories are the plan. What you grow indoors is **vitamin C, fresh
greens, live enzymes, variety, and morale** — the things that stored grain lacks
and that stop a 9-month confinement turning into scurvy, depression and illness.

That is not a consolation prize. **It is the exact gap that kills people on
storage diets**, and it is entirely solvable in a flat, in any season, in a few
square metres, with no soil and almost no power.

The second apartment truth: **your building is your land.** A block of forty
flats has a roof, a courtyard, a basement, a stairwell, and — most importantly —
forty households of skills, tools and labour. The single highest-value thing an
apartment dweller can do is organise the building (§10).

## 3. Sprouting — the apartment keystone

**Learn this technique this week. It is the most important thing in this chapter.**

Sprouting takes stored dry seed — which you already have, which keeps for years,
which weighs nothing — and converts it in **3–5 days** into fresh food with
**vitamin C that the dry seed did not have** (vitamin C is synthesised during
germination), plus greatly increased B vitamins, enzymes and digestibility. It
needs **no soil, no light, no power, no garden, and no season.**

> **Be clear about what it does:** sprouting does not create calories — dry matter
> actually falls slightly. It converts stored calories into **fresh nutrition**.
> That is exactly the gap in a long-term storage diet, and nothing else fills it
> so cheaply.

### Equipment (€20 total)
- 3–4 wide-mouth 1 L glass jars
- Mesh lids (or muslin and a rubber band)
- A rack or bowl to stand them at 45° to drain
- That's it.

### Method
1. **Rinse** 2–4 tablespoons (30–60 g) of seed, put it in the jar.
2. **Soak** 6–12 hours in plenty of cool water.
3. **Drain thoroughly** and invert the jar at 45° so it keeps draining.
4. **Rinse and drain twice a day** (three times in hot weather). Drainage is
   everything — standing water is what causes spoilage.
5. **Harvest at 2–5 days**, when the shoot is 1–3× the seed length.
6. Rinse, drain well, refrigerate or keep somewhere cool; eat within 3–5 days.
7. **Start a new jar every day.** Four jars on rotation = a continuous daily
   harvest, forever, from a bucket of seed.

### What to sprout, and expected yields (by weight)
| Seed | Days | Yield multiple | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mung bean** | 3–5 | **4–6×** | The workhorse. Bulky, mild, filling, cheap. |
| **Lentil** (whole brown/green) | 2–4 | 3–4× | Fast, reliable, nutty. Ordinary supermarket lentils work. |
| **Chickpea** | 3–4 | 3× | Hearty, good in salads and hummus |
| **Alfalfa** | 4–6 | **8–10×** | Highest yield by weight, very high vitamin |
| **Radish / mustard / cress** | 3–5 | 5–6× | Peppery, high vitamin C, fast |
| **Broccoli** | 3–5 | 5–6× | High in sulforaphane |
| **Fenugreek** | 3–5 | 4–5× | Medicinal, distinctive |
| **Wheat / barley / oat groats** | 2–3 | 2–3× | Sprouted grain for bread, or grow on for wheatgrass |
| **Sunflower (in shell)** | 2 days then grow on as shoots | — | Best as microgreens |
| ⚠️ **Never sprout** | — | — | **Kidney/red beans (toxic raw — must be boiled), tomato, potato, aubergine, pepper or rhubarb seed (nightshade/oxalate toxicity)** |

### Food safety (take this seriously)
Sprouts are grown warm and damp — which is also ideal for *Salmonella* and
*E. coli*, and sprouts have caused real outbreaks.
- Buy **seed intended for sprouting** where you can, and keep it dry and sealed.
- **Rinse thoroughly and drain completely, twice a day, every day.** Most failures
  are drainage failures.
- Keep jars at room temperature, not hot. Discard anything that smells sour,
  slimy or musty — trust your nose absolutely.
- Clean jars and lids between batches (boiling water).
- **If anyone in the household is pregnant, elderly, very young or
  immunocompromised, cook the sprouts** (stir-fried mung sprouts are excellent)
  rather than eating them raw.

### How much seed to store
**5 kg of mixed sprouting seed per person** covers a year of daily fresh food and
costs about **€25–40**. It stores 4+ years in sealed jars. Nothing else in this
entire plan delivers as much nutritional security per euro or per litre of
storage space. **Buy it this week.**

## 4. Microgreens — fresh greens in 10 days

Seedlings harvested at the first true-leaf stage. More substantial than sprouts,
genuinely delicious, and about as fast as food gets.

- **Tray:** any shallow container, 20 × 30 cm, with drainage. Stack of 4–6 trays
  on a shelf = a continuous supply.
- **Medium:** 2–3 cm of compost, coir, or even a folded damp paper towel or a
  hemp mat (no soil needed for most).
- **Seed:** sown **thickly** — 10–15 g per tray. Cover for the first 2–3 days
  (darkness encourages an even, leggy start), then uncover and give light.
- **Light:** a bright windowsill works; a **€20–30 LED strip at 20–40 W** works
  better and works in winter, running 12–16 h/day (≈ 300–600 Wh/day — plan for it
  in your power budget, or use the windowsill and accept slower growth).
- **Harvest at 7–14 days** with scissors, just above the medium. **150–250 g per
  tray.**
- **Best for volume and substance:** **pea shoots** and **sunflower shoots** —
  these are the most filling and the closest to a real vegetable. Then radish,
  broccoli, mustard, kale, beet, coriander, basil.
- **Reuse the compost** by adding it to a wormery or a balcony pot and refreshing it.
- **Pea shoots deserve special mention:** ordinary dried marrowfat/whole peas from
  the supermarket, soaked overnight and sown thickly, give a big tray of sweet
  shoots in 10 days, and will **regrow a second cut**. Cheapest fresh green there
  is.

## 5. Mushrooms — protein and texture from waste

Oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus ostreatus*) are the easiest and the most useful.

- **Substrate:** used coffee grounds, shredded cardboard, straw, sawdust, or
  paper. Pasteurise straw by soaking in hot (65–75 °C) water for an hour, or use
  fresh coffee grounds within 24 hours (already effectively pasteurised).
- **Spawn:** buy grain spawn (€10–20/kg). Mix ~5–10% spawn by weight into the
  substrate, pack into a bucket or bag with small holes.
- **Colonise** in a dark, warm-ish cupboard for 2–4 weeks (it goes white).
- **Fruit:** move to light and humidity (mist twice a day, or a bag with holes).
  Mushrooms appear over 5–10 days.
- **Yield:** 1 kg of substrate gives **200–400 g of mushrooms**, usually over 2–3
  flushes.
- **Spent substrate** goes to the wormery or the balcony pots.
- **You can propagate your own spawn** from a piece of a mushroom stem on damp
  cardboard — this is what makes it a **cycle** rather than a purchase.
- ⚠️ Only grow species you bought as spawn from a reputable supplier. **Never eat
  a wild or unknown mushroom, and never eat anything growing on your substrate
  that doesn't look like what you inoculated.** Contaminant moulds (green, black,
  pink) mean discard the batch outdoors.

## 6. Fermentation — the zero-power vitamin store

This is how Central and Eastern Europe survived winters for centuries, and it
needs nothing but vegetables, salt, a jar and a cupboard.

**Sauerkraut, the complete method:**
1. Shred 5 kg of cabbage.
2. Add **2% salt by weight** (100 g of salt for 5 kg of cabbage).
3. Massage/pound for 10 minutes until it releases its own brine.
4. Pack tightly into a crock or jars; **everything must stay below the brine**
   (use a weight, a cabbage leaf, or a water-filled bag).
5. Ferment at 18–20 °C for 1–3 weeks, burping jars or using an airlock.
6. Move to somewhere cold (cellar, unheated room, fridge). **Keeps 6–12 months.**

**Why it matters:** sauerkraut retains most of the cabbage's vitamin C *and*
becomes more digestible. Historically it is **the** anti-scurvy food of this
region. A 10 L crock is cheap, lasts forever, and is entirely power-independent.

Also ferment: cucumbers, carrots, beetroot, turnip, garlic, peppers, green
tomatoes, and hot sauce. And for the cupboard: sourdough starter (bread from
flour and water alone, no yeast purchase), vinegar (from wine, cider or scraps —
and vinegar is a preservative in its own right), and yoghurt/kefir if you have
milk (kefir grains multiply themselves indefinitely — a true renewable).

**Stock:** a 5–10 L fermenting crock or a dozen 1.5 L jars with airlocks,
several kg of non-iodised salt, and a weight for each vessel.

## 7. Balcony and window growing

**Accept the constraint and grow high-value, not high-calorie.**

| Crop | Container | Yield per season | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cherry tomatoes | 20–30 L pot | 2–4 kg per plant | ✅ Best value on a sunny balcony |
| Chillies & peppers | 10 L pot | 0.5–2 kg | ✅ Dry them; they keep forever and transform dull food |
| Cut-and-come-again salad | Window box | Weekly cuts for months | ✅ |
| Bush beans | 15 L pot | 0.3–0.5 kg | ✅ And they fix nitrogen |
| Radish | Window box | 4 week cycles | ✅ Fast morale |
| **Potatoes** | **40 L sack** | **1–2 kg** | ✅ The only real calories available |
| Herbs | Small pots | Continuous | ✅✅ Best value per cm² |
| Strawberries | Hanging basket | 0.5 kg | ✅ Perennial, propagates itself |
| Courgette | 30 L pot | 2–5 kg | ⚠️ Takes a lot of space |
| Dwarf/patio fruit tree | 40 L pot | 2–5 kg from year 3 | ✅ If you'll be there |

**Practical points**
- **Sun is the limit.** South/southwest facing, 6+ hours. A north-facing balcony
  will do salad, herbs and mushrooms and not much else — don't fight it, grow
  indoors instead.
- **Wind dries pots brutally** on a high balcony. Screen with mesh; use bigger
  pots (they dry more slowly); mulch the surface.
- **Weight:** wet compost is ~700 kg/m³. A row of large pots is a real structural
  load — keep them over the edge beams, near the wall, and check your building's
  rules. **Never overload a balcony.**
- **Self-watering containers** (a reservoir below the soil) halve your watering
  and survive a missed day. Easy to make from stacked boxes.
- **Reuse compost** by mixing in wormery castings, a handful of lime, and some
  fresh material each season. Store a couple of bags of compost — it is not
  something you can improvise in a flat.
- **Vertical:** railing planters, wall pockets, tiered shelves, hanging baskets.
  Trellis climbing beans and cucumbers up a string to the ceiling — vertical
  space is the one dimension a flat has plenty of.

## 8. Quail on a balcony — the realistic livestock

Reread Ch. 08 §5 for the details. In summary:
- **10 coturnix quail in 1 m²** (a 3-tier rabbit hutch or a purpose-built cage)
  give **8–9 eggs a day** — the protein equivalent of ~1.7 chicken eggs daily.
- **Laying from 6–8 weeks old.** Fastest protein of any livestock.
- Quiet, small, and legal in far more places than chickens — **but check your
  lease and local rules, and talk to your neighbours first.**
- Feed: game-bird crumb (20–24% protein), plus your microgreen trimmings, sprout
  surplus and mealworms. ~25–30 g/bird/day → 10 birds ≈ **100 kg of feed a year.**
  Store it.
- **They do not go broody** — renewal needs a small incubator (€60, ~40 W for
  17 days) and a heat plate for the chicks. That is a power dependency; plan for it.
- Droppings go to the wormery/balcony pots. Manage bedding well or the smell will
  end your neighbourly relations and your quail project together.

## 9. Apartment-specific failure modes

These are the things that make a flat different, and each needs a decision now.

| Problem | What happens | What to do about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Water pressure loss** | Buildings above ~5–8 floors are fed by electric booster pumps. **No power = no water above the low floors**, immediately. | Store aggressively (Ch. 01). Deploy the bath bladder at AMBER. Know the route to the ground-floor tap or the building's tank. |
| **No lift** | 20 L of water = 20 kg. A family needs 4 trips a day, up 8 floors. Groceries, waste, everything. | Store so you need fewer trips. A rope and a bucket from the balcony is not a joke — it is the practical answer. A folding sack truck helps on the flat. |
| **Sewer backup** | If mains pumping stations fail, or lower flats keep flushing, sewage backs up into the lowest flats. | **Stop flushing at the first sign.** Go to the bucket system (Ch. 05). Lower-floor residents: consider a drain plug/non-return valve. |
| **Waste with no collection** | A family generates ~1 m³ of rubbish a month, plus 100+ L of sanitation waste. It cannot stay in a flat. | The balcony drum, the building agreement, or the courtyard. **Decide in GREEN.** (Ch. 05 §3) |
| **Heating loss** | District heating and gas boilers both fail without power. A flat cools more slowly than a house (neighbours insulate you) but reaches the same place. | Warm room → warm *tent inside the room*. Layers, hot water bottles, duvets. **Never a flueless heater overnight.** (Ch. 04) |
| **No safe combustion** | You cannot run a generator, a charcoal stove or an unflued heater in a flat. Ever. CO kills in minutes in a sealed space. | Alcohol and gas stoves with ventilation, thermal cooker, and battery/solar power only. CO alarm mandatory. |
| **Fire** | Crisis cooking + candles + crowded rooms + no fire brigade. A flat fire spreads to neighbours. | Extinguisher + fire blanket + smoke alarm. Two exit routes known. Never leave a flame unattended. **This is a higher risk in a flat than in a house.** |
| **Smell, noise, light, smoke** | Your neighbours will know exactly what you have. Cooking smells travel through a stairwell; a generator or a lit window advertises. | Cook when others do. Blackout curtains. Discretion. (Ch. 10) |
| **Nowhere to store** | A year of food for four is ~1 tonne and 1.5–2 m³. | Under beds, wardrobe bases, above cupboards, behind the sofa, hallway cupboard, a rented storage unit, a cellar locker if the building has one. Dense, calorie-rich foods first. |
| **You cannot modify anything** | No wood stove, no well, no solar on the roof, no compost heap. | Plug-in and portable solutions only: power stations, folding/balcony panels, bucket systems, indoor growing. And **organise the building** (below). |
| **Everyone is very close** | Disease spreads through a block via shared stairwells, lifts, handrails and ventilation. | Mask in shared spaces, use the stairs not the lift, disinfect your own door handle, ventilate your flat, HEPA box in the shared room. (Ch. 06) |

## 10. The building is your land

**This is the apartment track's equivalent of a hectare, and it is the highest-value
thing you can do.** A block of flats has, collectively:

- **A roof** — rainwater catchment for the whole building, and possibly solar.
- **A courtyard, lawn or verge** — a communal garden, compost bays, a waste drum.
- **A basement or bike store** — bulk storage, a shared freezer, a workshop.
- **A stairwell** — a communication network.
- **Dozens of households** — with a nurse, an electrician, a plumber, a gardener,
  a carpenter and a lot of labour among them.

**Do this now, in GREEN:**
1. **Meet your immediate neighbours.** Names, numbers, who lives alone, who is
   elderly, who has medical needs, who has skills. This costs nothing and is worth
   more than any purchase in this plan.
2. **Propose the boring, popular things first**: a communal herb planter, a
   building WhatsApp/Signal group, a shared tool cupboard, a bulk-buying group.
   Nobody objects to these, and they build the relationships that matter later.
3. **Then the resilience items:** a rainwater butt on a downpipe, a compost bin, a
   shared generator or battery for the stairwell lights and phone charging, an
   agreed plan for waste if collections stop, a list of who checks on whom.
4. **Get it minuted.** A building committee decision in GREEN is worth ten
   arguments in RED.
5. **Share what you learn.** A neighbour with two weeks of food is a neighbour who
   is not knocking on your door in week three — and who might have the medicine
   you run out of in week nine.

## 12. Apartment checklist

```
[ ] I accept that stored food is the plan, and indoor growing is the nutrition
[ ] 5 kg of sprouting seed per person, plus jars — and I have sprouted a jar
[ ] Microgreen trays and seed in stock; one crop grown as practice
[ ] Fermenting crock + salt; one batch of sauerkraut made
[ ] Mushroom spawn and substrate plan
[ ] Bathtub bladder ready to deploy at AMBER
[ ] 200 L+ of water in jerry cans, stored where I can actually lift them
[ ] Hot water cylinder drain valve located and understood
[ ] Bucket toilet built and tested; 3 months of cover material stored
[ ] The destination for human waste is decided and agreed with the building
[ ] Power station + panels: enough for phones, lights, radio and medical devices
[ ] NO combustion device that could run unflued; CO alarm fitted
[ ] Fire extinguisher, fire blanket, smoke alarm; two exit routes known
[ ] Cooking possible with ventilation only: gas/alcohol + thermal cooker
[ ] 3 months of food stored in dense, calorie-rich form, inventoried
[ ] Balcony containers, compost and seed ready (if there is sun)
[ ] Quail considered, lease and neighbours checked
[ ] Neighbours' names and numbers known; building group exists
[ ] Building-level plan for water, waste and power discussed and minuted
[ ] A rural fallback contact identified
[ ] Blackout weekend drill completed in the flat, in winter
```

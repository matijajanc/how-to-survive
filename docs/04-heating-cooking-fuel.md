# 04 — Heating, Cooking & Fuel

*In a Central European winter, heat is a bigger energy problem than electricity,
food and water combined — by an order of magnitude. And the ways people improvise
heat and cooking indoors kill more people in a crisis than the crisis does.*

---

## 1. The scale of the problem

A typical Central European house loses **8,000–20,000 kWh of heat per winter**
(an uninsulated older house much more; a passive house a tenth of it). Compare
that with a 5 kWh battery.

```
Poorly insulated 120 m² house in January:  60–100 kWh/day of heat
A 10 kWh battery bank:                     10 kWh, once
A 3 kW solar array in January:              2.4 kWh/day
```

**You cannot heat a house with electricity in a crisis. Do not try.** Every
electric heater, oil-filled radiator and fan heater in your house is useless
without the grid, and running them off batteries or a generator is absurd —
2 kW for one room is more than most whole off-grid systems produce in a day.

The strategy has three parts, in strict order:

```
1. SHRINK the space you heat          (free, biggest win)
2. REDUCE the heat that escapes it    (cheap, second biggest win)
3. SUPPLY heat from stored fuel       (expensive, do last)
```

---

## 2. Step one: the warm room

**Heat one room, not a house.** This is how Europe lived for a thousand years and
it works.

- Pick the room: small, south-facing if possible, with a chimney or flue if you
  have one, away from the coldest walls, with a door that closes.
- **The whole household lives and sleeps there** in the coldest weeks. Mattresses
  on the floor. It is cramped, it is also warm, and bodies add heat (each person
  is ~80–100 W).
- Close doors to every other room. Close off the upstairs. Hang blankets over
  doorways and at the bottom of stairs (stairwells are chimneys).
- Keep the rest of the house **above freezing** (5 °C) to protect pipes, not
  comfortable. If you cannot, **drain the plumbing** in the unheated parts before
  the first hard frost: burst pipes flood a house you cannot get a plumber to.

**Result:** heating one 15 m² room instead of a 120 m² house cuts the fuel
requirement by **70–85%**. A whole winter becomes 2–4 m³ of wood instead of
15–20 m³. Nothing else you do comes close to this.

**The heated-bed trick:** a well-insulated bed with a hot water bottle (or a hot
stone/brick wrapped in cloth) needs *no room heat at all* to be comfortable.
Heating a body is a hundred times cheaper than heating a room. Layered wool
blankets, a duvet, a hat, and a hot water bottle at the feet will get you through
a 5 °C bedroom indefinitely.

---

## 3. Step two: cut the losses (cheap, do it this autumn)

| Measure | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Draught-proofing** — door seals, letterbox brush, keyhole covers, chimney balloon in unused flues, sealing around pipes and sockets | €50–100 | **10–20% of heat loss.** Best €/kWh in the building. |
| Heavy curtains, closed at dusk, opened at sunrise on sunny days | €50 or free (blankets) | 10–15% through windows |
| **Window insulation film** (shrink-fit) | €10–20/window | Turns single glazing into quasi-double. 20–30% of window loss. |
| Bubble wrap on windows you don't look through | €10 | Crude, surprisingly effective |
| Reflective foil behind radiators | €15 | 5% for radiators on external walls |
| Rugs and underlay on bare floors | Varies | Floors are 10–15% of loss in older houses |
| **Loft insulation to 300 mm** | €300–800 DIY | **20–25% of total heat loss.** The single best permanent investment. |
| Sealing and insulating the cellar hatch / floor void | €100 | 5–10% |
| Door snakes, thermal door curtains | €30 | Noticeable in a warm room |
| **Dressing properly**: base layer, wool jumper, hat, thick socks, slippers | €100 | Lets you hold the room at 16 °C instead of 21 °C — **a 30–40% fuel saving** |

The thermal comfort rule: **every 1 °C lower costs about 6–8% less fuel.**
Going from 21 °C to 17 °C saves roughly a quarter to a third of your heating fuel,
and with wool and a hot drink it is entirely liveable.

## 4. Step three: heat supply

### What happens to your existing heating without electricity?

Check this **today**, because most people are wrong about it:

| System | Works without mains power? |
|---|---|
| Gas boiler + radiators | ❌ **No.** Needs power for the pump, fan and controls. Dead in a blackout. |
| Gas boiler + gravity-fed system (old, rare) | ⚠️ Partly, if truly gravity circulated |
| Heat pump | ❌ No — and it is a large electrical load anyway |
| Pellet stove / pellet boiler | ❌ **No.** Needs power for the auger and fan. Also needs pellet deliveries. |
| Oil boiler | ❌ No (pump and burner are electric) |
| District heating | ❌ No, and entirely out of your control |
| **Wood stove / log burner** | ✅ **Yes** |
| **Masonry/tiled stove (kachelofen)** | ✅ Yes — and it stores heat for 12–24 h |
| **Open fireplace** | ⚠️ Works, but is 10–20% efficient and often *net negative* — it pulls more warm air up the chimney than it delivers |
| Bottled gas heater (catalytic/flueless) | ⚠️ Works, but consumes room oxygen and makes water vapour and CO — ventilation essential, never overnight |
| Paraffin/kerosene heater | ⚠️ Same caveats, plus fumes and fire risk |

**If your answer is ❌, you need a non-electric heat source before winter.**
A gas boiler can often be kept running with a very small battery/inverter
(the pump and controls may only be 50–150 W → 1.2–3.6 kWh/day) — check yours and
size for it. That is often the cheapest fix for an existing house.

### Wood — the backbone for the land track

**Energy content:** seasoned hardwood ≈ **4 kWh/kg**, or roughly
**1,800–2,100 kWh per stacked m³** (beech/oak/hornbeam at the top, spruce/pine
around 1,300–1,500 kWh/m³ and burning faster).

**How much you need per winter:**

| Scenario | Wood |
|---|---|
| Warm-room only, one efficient stove, 4–5 months | **2 – 4 m³** |
| Whole small house, well insulated | 6 – 10 m³ |
| Whole older house, poorly insulated | 15 – 25 m³ |
| Cooking only, year-round, on a rocket/efficient stove | 1 – 2 m³ |

**Seasoning is the thing people get wrong.** Freshly cut wood is 45–60% water.
Burning it gives you **half the heat**, coats your chimney with creosote (chimney
fire), and produces choking smoke.
- Split, stacked, covered on top but **open at the sides**, off the ground.
- **Hardwood: 18–24 months. Softwood: 9–12 months.**
- Target **< 20% moisture** — buy a €15 moisture meter and actually check.
- **This means you must start two years before you need it.** Wood is the one
  preparation that cannot be bought at AMBER. Buy or cut this year's wood *and*
  next year's, and keep a rolling two-year stack forever.

**Stoves:** a modern clean-burn wood stove is 70–80% efficient versus 10–20% for
an open fire. **Buy one with a cooktop surface** — then the same fuel heats the
room, cooks the food, heats water in a kettle and dries clothes. That
multi-functionality is worth more than a few percent of efficiency.
- Installation must be legal and safe: correct flue, register plate, hearth,
  clearances to combustibles, a swept and lined chimney. Get it signed off.
- **Sweep the chimney at least annually**, more with soft or damp wood. Buy your
  own rod set (€50) — you will not get a sweep in a crisis.
- **A masonry/tiled stove (kachelofen)** stores 100+ kWh in its mass and releases
  it for 12–24 hours from one or two firings. If you are building or renovating,
  it is the best cold-climate heat storage that exists.
- **Rocket mass heater:** a DIY design that burns small wood extremely cleanly and
  stores the heat in a cob/masonry bench. Very fuel-efficient and cheap to build,
  but bulky, non-standard and often not compliant with building regulations —
  check before building.

**Sourcing wood without a forest:** pallets (untreated/HT only — **never** MB
methyl-bromide-treated or painted), tree surgeons' arisings (often free if you
collect), storm-fall, council permits for gathering, coppice agreements with a
neighbour, offcuts from a joinery. Build the relationships in GREEN.

**Tools:** bow saw and a good axe **before** a chainsaw (they always work).
Then chainsaw + PPE (helmet, ear and eye protection, chaps/trousers, gloves),
files, chain oil, 2 spare chains, and 2-stroke fuel. A log splitter or a splitting
maul and wedges. **Chainsaw injuries are the classic homestead disaster — get
training.**

### Bottled gas — the backbone for the apartment track and the best bridge fuel

- **Propane (LPG) stores indefinitely**, needs no stabiliser, and delivers about
  **13.8 kWh/kg** — an **11 kg cylinder ≈ 150 kWh**.
- For **cooking only**, an 11 kg cylinder lasts a family roughly **6–10 weeks**.
  Four cylinders ≈ 6–9 months of cooking. This is a very achievable store.
- For **heating**, gas goes fast: a 4 kW heater burns an 11 kg cylinder in
  ~37 hours of running. Gas is a cooking fuel and a short-term heat fuel, not a
  winter heating strategy.
- Store cylinders **outside or in a ventilated outbuilding**, upright, away from
  drains and cellars (LPG is heavier than air and pools). Many leases and building
  rules limit or forbid cylinders in flats — check, and if forbidden, use small
  butane canisters and an alcohol stove instead.
- Butane (the blue camping canisters) **stops vaporising below about 0 °C** —
  useless outdoors in winter. Propane works to −40 °C. For cold-weather use, buy
  propane or propane/butane mixes.

## 5. Cooking without the grid

**Rank your cooking options by fuel independence:**

| Method | Fuel | Indoor safe? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wood stove with cooktop** | Wood | ✅ (properly flued) | The champion: heat + cooking + water + drying from one fire |
| **Rocket stove** | Twigs, sticks, offcuts | ❌ Outdoor/covered only | Astonishingly efficient — boils 1 L on a handful of sticks. €30 to buy, €0 to build from a tin and firebricks. **Every land household should have one.** |
| **Propane camping/caravan stove** | LPG | ⚠️ With good ventilation | Fast, controllable, clean. The apartment default. |
| **Butane canister stove** | Butane | ⚠️ With ventilation | Cheap (€20), canisters are compact. Fails in the cold. |
| **Alcohol / meths / Trangia** | Bioethanol, denatured alcohol | ⚠️ The safest liquid fuel indoors (burns to CO₂ and water, no soot), still ventilate | Slow, quiet, invisible flame — mark the stove. Fuel stores indefinitely. |
| **Thermal cooker / haybox** | *Almost none* | ✅ | **Boil 10–15 min, then insulate for 3–6 h.** Cuts fuel 50–80% on stews, beans, grains, soups. €70 to buy or free to make (a box, a blanket, a duvet, straw). **Buy or build one — it multiplies every other fuel you own.** |
| **Pressure cooker** | Any | ✅ | Cuts cooking time and fuel by 50–70%. Beans in 30 min instead of 2 h. |
| **Solar oven** | Sunlight | ✅ (outdoors) | Free, silent, no smoke, no fuel. 120–150 °C on a sunny day — bakes bread, cooks stews and rice, pasteurises water. Weather-dependent, and useless in a Central European winter. €50 to build from card, foil and glass. |
| **Charcoal / BBQ** | Charcoal | ❌ **NEVER INDOORS** | Charcoal produces enormous quantities of CO with no smoke to warn you. It kills whole families every year. Outdoors only, always. |
| **Open fire** | Wood | ❌ Outdoor | Wasteful, smoky, visible, smellable. Last resort. |
| **Electric induction/hob** | Electricity | ✅ | Only while the grid is up |

**The fuel-efficient cooking doctrine** (this doubles or triples your fuel store):
1. **Soak** beans, chickpeas and grains overnight — cuts cooking time by half.
2. **Pressure cook** whatever you can.
3. **Bring to the boil hard, then transfer to the thermal cooker** for the long
   simmer. This is the single biggest fuel saving available.
4. **Lids on, always.** Uncovered pots waste 2–3× the energy.
5. **Match the pot to the burner**; a small pot on a big flame wastes most of it.
6. **Batch cook** — one fire, several meals. Cook tomorrow's food with today's fire.
7. **Use the residual heat**: bread, then a casserole, then dried herbs, then a
   hot water bottle, all from one stove firing.
8. **Windshield** any outdoor stove — wind can double fuel use.
9. **Eat one-pot food.** Stews, porridge, soup, dal. Fewer pots, less washing
   water, less fuel.

**Baking without an oven:** a Dutch oven / cast-iron pot with a lid on a wood
stove or in embers; a stove-top "bread pot"; a solar oven; a hot-box. Flatbreads,
tortillas and pan breads need only a frying pan — learn these, they are the
cheapest bread in fuel terms.

## 6. Carbon monoxide: the thing that will actually kill you

More people die from improvised indoor heating and cooking in a crisis than from
almost any other cause. CO is **colourless, odourless, tasteless**, and the early
symptoms — headache, nausea, sleepiness, confusion — are exactly the symptoms
that stop you realising you need to act.

**The absolute rules:**
- **NEVER** burn charcoal, a BBQ, a generator, a petrol engine, or a patio heater
  indoors, in a garage, in a cellar, in a tent, in a porch, or in a caravan.
  Not with a window open. Not "just for a bit".
- **NEVER** use a gas cooker as a room heater.
- **NEVER** sleep with a flueless combustion appliance running.
- **Any flame consumes oxygen and produces CO₂, water vapour and some CO.**
  Ventilate — a 2–5 cm window gap is a small heat cost and a large safety gain.
- **Fit CO alarms** (EN 50291): one in every sleeping room, one in any room with
  a combustion appliance, one near the generator's nearest wall. **€20 each.**
  Check them monthly, replace every 5–7 years. This is the cheapest life
  insurance in this entire plan.
- Fit **smoke alarms** too, and keep **a fire extinguisher (ABC powder, 2 kg) and
  a fire blanket** in the kitchen and by the stove. Crisis cooking on unfamiliar
  appliances in a crowded warm room is exactly how house fires start, and there
  may be no fire brigade coming.
- If anyone gets a headache, feels sick or sleepy when the heater is on and
  better when outside: **get everyone out, ventilate, do not go back in.**

## 7. Hot water

- **On the stove.** A kettle or a pot living permanently on the wood stove
  provides washing water all day for free.
- **Wood stove back boiler** — heats a cylinder as a by-product of room heating.
  Must be correctly installed with a heat leak/dump radiator and pressure relief,
  or it is a bomb. Use a professional.
- **Solar shower bag** (20 L, €15): black PVC bag on a sunny wall reaches 40 °C in
  a few hours, April–September. The cheapest hot water there is.
- **Solar thermal panel / batch heater**: a genuinely good investment in a sunny
  climate; a DIY batch heater (a black tank in a glazed box) costs €100.
- Wash with much less: a basin, a flannel and 3 L of hot water gets a person
  properly clean.

## 8. Build sequence

### 🏢 Apartment
1. **Now (€120):** 2-burner butane/propane camping stove + 8 canisters ·
   CO alarm · fire blanket · thermal cooker or a DIY haybox · warm bedding and
   base layers for everyone.
2. **L1 (€250):** + 4 more canisters or a small propane cylinder if permitted ·
   alcohol stove + 5 L of fuel (safest indoor option) · window film ·
   heavy curtains · draught excluders · sleeping bags rated to 0 °C.
3. **L2 (€400):** + a pressure cooker · more fuel (target 3 months of cooking:
   ~10 kg of propane or 20 butane canisters) · a room-in-a-room tent for winter
   (a cheap 4-person tent inside the living room genuinely works — it traps body
   heat and turns an unheated flat into a survivable one).
4. **Never** a generator, charcoal, or a flueless heater overnight. In a flat you
   cannot make CO safe; you can only avoid making it.

### 🏡 Land / house
1. **Now (€150):** CO + smoke alarms · fire extinguisher + blanket ·
   draught-proofing · thermal cooker · rocket stove.
2. **L1 (€800–2,500):** wood stove with cooktop, professionally installed and
   signed off · 2 m³ of seasoned wood · chimney rods · moisture meter ·
   2 × 11 kg propane + regulator and hoses · bow saw, axe, maul.
3. **L2 (€1,500–4,000):** + loft insulation · 4–6 m³ seasoned wood (this year's
   and next year's) · chainsaw + full PPE + spares · log store built ·
   solar shower or back boiler · warm-room prepared and tested.
4. **L3:** masonry stove or rocket mass heater · two-year rolling wood supply ·
   coppice or woodland access · solar thermal · full-house insulation upgrade.

## 9. Heating & fuel checklist

```
[ ] I know exactly what happens to my heating in a blackout
[ ] A non-electric heat source exists for at least one room
[ ] Warm room chosen, prepared, and slept in for one test night in winter
[ ] Draught-proofing, curtains and window film done before autumn
[ ] Loft insulated to 300 mm
[ ] Wood: this winter's supply seasoned AND next winter's stacked
[ ] Moisture meter owned; wood measured under 20%
[ ] Chimney swept this year; own rods in stock
[ ] Cooking: at least TWO fuel-independent methods available
[ ] Thermal cooker / haybox owned and used
[ ] Pressure cooker owned and used
[ ] 3 months of cooking fuel stored (propane preferred)
[ ] Fuel stored legally, outdoors, upright, ventilated
[ ] CO alarms in every sleeping room and near every combustion appliance
[ ] Smoke alarms tested; extinguisher and fire blanket in place
[ ] Everyone in the household knows the "never indoors" rule by heart
[ ] Winter bedding, wool layers, hats and hot water bottles for every person
[ ] Unheated-zone plumbing can be drained before a hard frost
[ ] Cold-night drill completed
```

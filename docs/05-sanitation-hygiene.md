# 05 — Sanitation & Hygiene

*Historically, in every siege, refugee camp, flood and collapse, more people die
of dysentery, cholera and typhoid than of anything else. This chapter is not
optional and it is not glamorous. It is the one that keeps your family alive
while everyone else's plan is about food.*

---

## 1. The problem, stated plainly

Each person produces, every day:
- **~1.2–1.5 litres of urine** (sterile when fresh, smelly and ammoniacal within
  hours)
- **~150–250 g of faeces** (containing, if that person is ill, billions of
  infectious organisms)
- **Grey water** from washing
- **Household waste**

A family of four produces roughly **6 L of urine and 800 g of faeces per day**,
every day, for as long as the crisis lasts. Over three months that is **540 L of
urine and 72 kg of faeces.** You must have a plan for it before day one.

**And the failure mode is fast:** one person with a gastrointestinal infection,
one unwashed pair of hands, one fly walking from the bucket to the bread, and the
whole household is ill. Diarrhoea while isolated, with a saturated health system,
in an unheated house, with limited water, is genuinely life-threatening —
especially for children and the elderly.

**The controls, in order of importance:**
```
1. Keep hands clean          (the single biggest intervention)
2. Keep faeces away from food, water and flies
3. Keep clean water clean    (see Ch. 01)
4. Keep sick people separate (see Ch. 06)
5. Wash and dry things
```

## 2. Handwashing — the highest-value habit in a crisis

Handwashing with soap cuts diarrhoeal disease by **30–50%** and respiratory
infection meaningfully too. It is more effective than any item you can buy.

**When:** after the toilet, after handling waste or animals, after being outside
or handling deliveries, before preparing food, before eating, before and after
caring for anyone ill, after coughing or sneezing.

**How, with almost no water:**
- **Tippy-tap:** a 5 L jug or bottle on a string, pivoted by a foot lever, pouring
  a thin stream. Costs nothing, uses **0.2 L per wash**, and — crucially — you
  never touch the container with dirty hands. Build one, indoors or out.
- **Tap-fitted jerry can** (a 20 L can with a spigot on a shelf) — same effect.
- Wet, lather **20 seconds**, scrub between fingers and under nails, rinse into a
  greywater bucket. Air-dry or use a dedicated personal towel.
- **Soap matters more than water volume.** Even cold, muddy water with soap beats
  clean water without.
- Alcohol hand rub (≥ 60%) is a supplement, not a substitute — it does not work
  well on visibly dirty hands and is poor against norovirus. Have both.

**Stock soap generously.** It is cheap, light, stores for years (bar soap
improves with age as it hardens), and is an outstanding barter good. **Target
1 bar per person per month, plus 5 L of liquid soap and 5 L of washing-up
liquid, per year.** Learn to make soap from lye + rendered fat as an L3 skill.

## 3. Toilets without mains water

### First: can you still flush?
If water supply fails but the **sewer/septic still works**, you can flush with
any water — greywater, rainwater, pool water, stream water.
- Pour **4–6 L briskly straight into the bowl** (not the cistern) to trigger the
  siphon. Use the cistern only if you have water to spare.
- Practise "**if it's yellow, let it mellow**" — flush solids only.
- **Warning:** if you live in a block of flats and the sewer or the building's
  pumping station backs up, flushing pushes sewage into the lowest flats.
  If there is any sign of backup, or if the mains sewer depends on electric
  pumping stations in your area, **stop flushing immediately** and go to the
  bucket system.

### The bucket (sawdust / "humanure") toilet — the household standard
Reliable, cheap, odour-free when done properly, and usable indoors. This is the
answer for **both tracks**.

**Build it:**
- Two or more **20 L buckets** (one in use, one being emptied/held).
- A **toilet seat with a lid** on a simple plywood box, or a purpose-made
  bucket-toilet seat (€25).
- A bin of **cover material** and a scoop.

**Use it:**
1. Put 3–5 cm of cover material in the bottom of an empty bucket.
2. Use the toilet as normal (urine and faeces together is fine, but see
   separation below).
3. **Cover completely after every single use** — a generous handful of material
   until nothing is visible. This is the whole trick: a covered deposit does not
   smell, does not attract flies, and composts aerobically.
4. Keep the lid down between uses.
5. When two-thirds full, seal and swap to the next bucket.

**Cover material — stock this deliberately, you need a lot of it:**
sawdust (not from treated or painted wood) · wood shavings · shredded dry leaves ·
coconut coir (compressed bricks — compact to store, ideal for flats) ·
shredded paper and cardboard · peat-free compost · chopped straw · dry garden
soil · rice hulls.
**Plan ~1–2 L of cover material per person per day → roughly 150–250 L per
person per three months.** Coir bricks are the apartment answer: one 5 kg brick
expands to ~70 L and stores on a shelf.

**Urine separation** transforms the system. Urine is ~90% of the volume and
almost all of the smell when mixed. A urine-diverting seat (€40–80), or simply a
separate bottle/jug for urine, means:
- The solids bucket fills **5× more slowly** and barely smells.
- Much less cover material is needed.
- The urine is a superb, sterile nitrogen fertiliser (see below).
Men and children can use a dedicated bottle; a urine-diverting insert handles
everyone.

### Where does it go?

**🏡 Land track — compost it.**
- Build **two bays** of 1 m³ each from pallets, on soil, sited away from any
  water source and downhill from wells.
- Start with a 30 cm base of straw or wood chips to absorb liquid.
- Empty the bucket into the centre of the pile, then **cover immediately and
  completely** with straw, leaves or chips. A properly covered humanure pile has
  no odour and no flies. Never leave deposited material uncovered.
- **Fill one bay for a year, then leave it sealed and undisturbed for a second
  full year** while you fill the other. Two years total is the conservative
  standard used by experienced practitioners.
- A thermophilic pile reaching 50–60 °C kills pathogens in days, but piles built
  in cold weather from small daily additions often don't get there — which is why
  the **long rest period is the real safety margin**, not the temperature.
- **Use the finished compost on fruit trees, berries, ornamentals and
  grain/fodder crops. Do not use it on root vegetables, salads, or anything eaten
  raw.** Be conservative; this is one place where caution costs nothing.
- Wash hands, and keep a dedicated pair of gloves and a boot brush for the job.

**🏡 Land track — the alternative: trench latrine.**
Faster and simpler if you have space and are not staying forever.
- **At least 30 m from any well, spring or watercourse, and downhill from it.**
- 30 cm wide, 1 m deep, as long as needed. Throw a scoop of soil in after each use;
  backfill and mound over when 30 cm from the top, and mark it.
- Not for winter (frozen ground) and not for high water tables.

**🏢 Apartment track — the hard problem.**
This is the single toughest apartment challenge and it must be solved in advance.
Options, in order of preference:
1. **Sewer still works** → flush with greywater. Easy. Most likely scenario.
2. **Building agreement:** a sealed 120–200 L drum in a bin store, courtyard or
   basement, emptied by the council or a contracted service when collections
   resume; or a communal composting point in a courtyard garden. **Agree this
   with neighbours and the building committee in GREEN, not in RED.** Several
   flats cooperating can manage this; one flat alone cannot.
3. **Double-bagging:** heavy-duty bin liners inside the bucket, tied off, stored
   in a sealed outer drum on a balcony until collections resume. Add cover
   material and a scoop of garden lime or wood ash to control odour.
   Realistic for weeks, unpleasant for months, and it depends on collections
   eventually returning.
4. **Urine:** dilute 1:8 with water and pour down a still-working drain or onto
   balcony plants/trees below (ask first). Undiluted urine burns plants.
5. **Never** dump waste in a stairwell, lift shaft, rubbish chute or watercourse.
   That is how a building becomes uninhabitable and how an outbreak starts.

**Urine as fertiliser (both tracks):** fresh urine from a healthy person is
sterile and is an excellent nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium fertiliser.
**Dilute 1:8 to 1:10 with water** and apply to soil around growing plants (not on
leaves, not on anything about to be eaten raw, and stop 3–4 weeks before harvest).
One adult's annual urine contains roughly enough nitrogen for **200–300 m² of
vegetable garden** — a genuinely significant free input when fertiliser is
unavailable. Don't use urine from anyone on medication you'd rather not
concentrate in your soil.

## 4. Washing and laundry

**Body washing** — see Ch. 01 §6. A basin, a flannel, 3 L of warm water and soap
gets a person genuinely clean. A weekly proper wash plus daily face/hands/armpits/
groin/feet is the standard that prevents skin infections and keeps morale up.
**Do not let washing lapse.** Feeling filthy is corrosive to morale in a way that
compounds everything else, and skin infections in a crisis are dangerous.

**Laundry without a machine:**
- **Two-bucket method:** wash bucket with hot soapy water, rinse bucket with cool
  clean water. A **plunger-style washer** (a "breathing mobile washer", €25) or a
  clean toilet plunger with holes drilled in it makes agitation far less
  exhausting.
- Or a **bucket + lid + plunger hole** as a manual "washing machine"; or a
  hand-cranked camping washer (€60–100).
- A **mop wringer or a hand mangle** removes far more water than wringing by
  hand, and drying is the bottleneck in winter.
- Budget **~30 L per load**; reuse the rinse water as the next wash water.
- **Dry indoors in the warm room with ventilation**, or outside. Wet laundry in a
  sealed room causes condensation, mould and respiratory problems — always vent.
- Wash **underwear, socks and anything soiled frequently**; outer layers rarely.
  Air clothes instead of washing them. Wool needs washing far less often than
  cotton and stays warm when damp — build the household's clothing around it.
- **Stock:** washing soda, bar soap or laundry soap, a scrubbing brush, a
  washboard (€15, genuinely useful), spare pegs and line, and enough clothing that
  laundry can lag by a week.

**Dishes:** scrape first, two-basin wash + rinse, air-dry (drying cloths spread
bacteria). Add a splash of bleach to the rinse water if anyone is ill.

## 5. The supplies people forget

| Item | Quantity for 4 people, 12 months | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Toilet paper** | ~150–200 rolls | Bulky. Or reduce with a **bidet bottle** (a €3 squeeze bottle or a garden sprayer) — uses 0.5 L and cuts paper use by 80%. **Family cloth** (dedicated washable cloths, laundered hot) is the zero-consumable option. |
| **Soap** | 50 bars + 5 L liquid | Also a top-tier barter good |
| **Washing-up liquid / laundry soap** | 10 L / 10 kg | |
| **Menstrual supplies** | **Critical, and constantly forgotten.** | **Menstrual cups (€25 each, last 5–10 years) and reusable cloth pads are the strategic answer** — they remove the problem permanently. Also keep 12 months of disposables as a buffer and for others. |
| **Nappies/diapers** | 12 months | Reusable cloth nappies + liners + a soaking bucket. Disposables for a year are ~2,000 units and simply won't fit in a flat. |
| **Toothbrushes & toothpaste** | 8 brushes, 12 tubes | Dental problems in a crisis are agony with no dentist. **Bicarbonate of soda + salt works as toothpaste**; store a few kg. Floss (also useful as strong thread). |
| **Bin liners, heavy duty** | 200+ | Waste, sanitation, waterproofing |
| **Bleach / disinfectant** | 10 L | Cleaning and water treatment. Rotate annually. |
| **Gloves (nitrile)** | 500 | Sanitation, medical, cleaning |
| **Paper towel / rags** | Plenty of old cotton cut into rags | Washable and reusable |
| **Hair clippers (manual)** | 1 | Hygiene and morale |
| **Nail clippers, razors, comb** | Per person | Ingrown nails and untreated cuts get infected |
| **Insect screens / mesh** | For windows and the compost | Fly control is disease control |

## 6. Waste, flies and rodents

**Waste reduction comes first** — with no collections, whatever you bring in you
keep. Buy in bulk with less packaging, compost all food waste, burn clean paper
and card (land track), and separate what will eventually be collected.

**Organic waste** → compost heap (land) or a small bokashi bucket / wormery
(apartment — a wormery under a sink handles a family's vegetable waste silently
and produces fertiliser for the balcony).

**Non-compostable waste** → sealed bins, kept outside the living space, well away
from food and water. Rinse anything food-contaminated before storing it.

**Flies are the vector that connects faeces to food.** Control them:
- Cover every deposit of waste, always.
- Screens on windows and food covers on the table.
- Fly traps and papers; keep a stock.
- Keep the compost properly covered with a carbon layer.

**Rodents** eat your food store, chew wiring, and carry leptospirosis, hantavirus
and salmonella. Control them:
- Food in **metal or thick plastic bins**, never in sacks on the floor.
- Snap traps (stock 12+) and keep the area clear of nesting material.
- Block entry points with steel wool and mortar.
- A cat earns its food.
- Use poison very cautiously if you have pets, children or eat the local wildlife.

**Medical/infectious waste** (masks, tissues, dressings from a sick room) →
double-bagged, sealed, separate, ideally burned (land) or held apart (flat).
See Ch. 06.

## 7. Disinfection — what concentration for what

Household bleach (5–6% sodium hypochlorite), diluted **fresh** (chlorine
solutions lose strength within ~24 hours):

| Use | Dilution | Contact time |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking water | 2 drops per litre | 30 min |
| **Surfaces, general cleaning** | **1:100** (10 mL per litre) | 1 min |
| **Body fluids, vomit, faeces, sick-room surfaces** | **1:10** (100 mL per litre) | 10 min |
| Soaking dishes/cloths from a sick person | 1:100 | 10 min |
| Boots/footbath at a threshold | 1:50 | — |

- **Never mix bleach with ammonia, acids, or toilet cleaners** — it releases
  chlorine or chloramine gas and it will hurt you badly in a small room.
- Ventilate while using it.
- Alternatives: 70% alcohol (surfaces, fast, flammable), hydrogen peroxide 3%,
  boiling water (dishes, cloths), soap and mechanical scrubbing (always the first
  step — disinfectant on a dirty surface does little).
- **Wood ash and lime** are the zero-supply-chain options: wood ash is alkaline
  and mildly disinfectant, useful thrown on latrine contents; garden lime
  (calcium hydroxide) controls odour and pathogens in pits and drums.

## 8. Build sequence

### 🏢 Apartment
1. **Now (€60):** 2 × 20 L buckets + a bucket-toilet seat · 2 coir bricks ·
   a bidet bottle · 5 kg of soap · 200 bin liners · nitrile gloves · a tippy-tap
   or a jerry can with a spigot.
2. **L1 (€150):** + urine-diverting insert or dedicated urine bottles ·
   4 more coir bricks · menstrual cups · plunger washer + 2 buckets ·
   fly screens · 5 L bleach · washing soda.
3. **L2 (€250):** + 3 months of cover material (8–10 coir bricks) · a sealed
   120 L outer drum for the balcony · lime or wood ash · reusable nappies if
   relevant · **a written agreement with the building/neighbours about where
   waste goes.**

### 🏡 Land / house
1. **Now (€80):** as apartment L0, plus a spade and a designated latrine site.
2. **L1 (€200):** + two pallet compost bays built · a sawdust/shavings supply
   (a trailer-load from a sawmill is often free) · straw bales · washboard and
   mangle · outdoor handwashing station.
3. **L2 (€400):** + a dedicated outhouse or a purpose-built composting toilet ·
   greywater line to the orchard · a year of cover material under cover ·
   solar shower · rodent-proof metal bins for the food store.
4. **L3:** an established two-bay humanure rotation in its second year ·
   soap-making from lye and tallow · a wormery/bokashi system for kitchen waste ·
   a reed bed or mulch basin for greywater.

## 9. Sanitation checklist

```
[ ] Bucket toilet built and TESTED (actually used for a weekend)
[ ] 3 months of cover material stored and dry
[ ] Urine separation arranged
[ ] The destination for waste is decided, legal, and agreed with neighbours
[ ] Compost bays built and sited >30 m from any water source, downhill (land)
[ ] Handwashing station exists at the toilet and at the kitchen
[ ] Soap stocked: 1 bar/person/month for 12 months
[ ] Bidet bottle or family cloth as a paper-reduction plan
[ ] Menstrual cups / reusable pads bought BEFORE they are needed
[ ] Reusable nappies if there is or may be an infant
[ ] Laundry method tested by hand-washing a full load
[ ] Drying plan for winter that does not cause condensation
[ ] Bleach stocked and in date; dilution table printed and posted
[ ] Fly screens, fly traps, food covers
[ ] Rodent-proof food storage; 12 traps
[ ] Waste separation and storage plan for no collections
[ ] ORS in the medical kit and everyone knows the recipe
[ ] Gloves, dedicated boots and a hand-hygiene routine for waste handling
```

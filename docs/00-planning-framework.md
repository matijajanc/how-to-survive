# 00 — The Planning Framework

*How to plan, in what order, with what money, and how to know when to act.*

Most people "prepare" by buying things they saw in a video. Six months later they
own a water filter, forty tins of beans, and a torch, and they still die of
hypothermia or dysentery. This chapter is the method that prevents that.

---

## 1. Build the threat model before the shopping list

Write down, on one page, the **specific** disruptions you are planning against.
For a severe pandemic, the realistic list is:

| Disruption | Likelihood | Duration | Effect on you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supply chain gaps, empty shelves for specific goods | Near certain | 1–12 months, intermittent | Can't buy flour, oil, medicine, spare parts |
| Voluntary or mandated isolation | Near certain | Weeks to months at a time | Can't leave; can't work; deliveries stop |
| Health system saturation | Near certain | 3–18 months | *Ordinary* emergencies become lethal |
| Price inflation / currency stress | Likely | 1–3 years | Your savings buy less; cash-only periods |
| Rolling electricity blackouts | Likely | Hours to days at a time | Fridge, freezer, heating pumps, water pumps, comms |
| Water pressure loss or boil-water notices | Possible | Days to weeks | You need stored + treatable water |
| Waste collection suspended | Likely | Weeks | Sanitation and vermin problems |
| Fuel rationing / stations closed | Possible | Weeks | No generator fuel, no vehicle, no deliveries |
| Complete grid failure > 1 week | Unlikely but catastrophic | — | Everything at once |
| Civil disorder in your area | Location-dependent | Days | Security becomes primary |

**Your plan's job is to make each row survivable.** Not to make you invincible.
Print the table, mark your current answer for each row, and you have your
priority list for the next six months.

### What you are NOT planning for
Be explicit about this too, because it saves money and sanity. You are not
planning for permanent civilisational collapse, nuclear war, or defending a
compound against an army. If that ever happens, the plan is "leave with the
go-bag and find people" — not "hold the house". Everything here is sized for
**a bad decade, not the end of the world.**

## 2. Audit your household honestly

Fill in [`worksheets/household-audit.md`](../worksheets/household-audit.md).
The key outputs are:

**a) People.** Number, ages, weights, and for each person: chronic conditions,
prescription medicines (name, dose, how many days you hold), allergies,
mobility limits, glasses/dental needs, pregnancy or infants, dietary needs, and
pets (they eat too — 12 months of dog food is 100+ kg).

**b) Calorie and water demand.** Sum the daily requirement for your actual
household. Don't use "a person" — use *your* people (see Ch. 03 and Ch. 01).

**c) The building.** Own or rent? House or flat? Which floor? Heating type and
what happens to it without electricity (most gas boilers and all pellet stoves
are dead without power). Insulation quality. Roof area and material (rainwater
potential). Is there a cellar, a garage, a loft, a south-facing roof or balcony?
Water: mains only, or well/borehole/spring/stream? Sewer or septic?

**d) The land.** Total m², usable growing m², sun hours, slope, soil type, water
access, existing trees, fencing, outbuildings.

**e) Skills in the household.** Who can garden, cook from raw ingredients, sew,
weld, do first aid, fix a bicycle, wire 12 V, butcher an animal, shoot, speak to
neighbours, keep calm. List the gaps — those are your training plan.

**f) Current endurance, in days.** For each category, how many days could you
last starting right now with the doors locked:

```
Water          ___ days      Sanitation     ___ days
Food           ___ days      Medicine       ___ days
Heat           ___ days      Power          ___ days
Cooking fuel   ___ days      Hygiene/soap   ___ days
```

**Your real preparedness is the lowest number on that list.** Not the average.
Fix the smallest number first, every time. This single rule will direct your
spending better than any list.

## 3. Set targets by level, not by wishlist

Work through the four levels from the README **horizontally** — every category to
L1 before any category to L2.

### L0 — Ready (72 hours) · one weekend · €250–500
The legal-minimum civil-defence standard. Everyone should be here already.
- 30 L water per person stored + a means to treat more
- 3 days of food needing no cooking
- Torches, batteries, a radio, a power bank
- First aid kit, 2 weeks of any prescription meds
- Cash in small notes; documents in one bag
- A way to keep warm with no heating

### L1 — Buffered (2 weeks) · one month · €800–1,800
Covers the overwhelming majority of real disruptions.
- 200 L water/person stored or a reliable treatable source
- 14 days of normal food you actually eat + cooking fuel for it
- A camping stove + fuel; a way to heat one room
- Lighting and phone charging independent of the grid (small solar)
- Full medicine chest, 30+ days of prescriptions
- Bucket toilet kit and hygiene supplies for two weeks

### L2 — Isolated (3 months) · 3–6 months · €3,000–8,000
The core of pandemic preparedness — the "don't leave the house for a season" level.
- 3 months of staples, packed for long storage, with rotation started
- A power system that runs the fridge/freezer, lights and comms indefinitely
- A water source you control (rainwater, well, or 600 L+/person stored) with
  multi-stage filtration and disinfectant chemicals for 3+ months
- Wood/propane for 3 months of cooking and one heated room
- Sanitation fully solved without mains water
- A pandemic isolation protocol, PPE, and a 3-month medicine buffer
- First growing season started, first animals if you have land

### L3 — Self-renewing (12 months + cycles) · 1–3 years · €8,000–25,000
- 12 months of stored calories *as a buffer, not the plan*
- Food production with saved seed, breeding animals, and made compost
- Energy: enough solar/battery for year-round critical loads + 2 winters of wood
- Water: rainwater capture + storage sized for your dry season, or a hand-pumped well
- Preservation capacity: pressure canner, jars, dehydrator, root cellar, smoker
- Spares, tools and consumables to keep all of the above running for a year
- A functioning mutual-aid group

## 4. Budget: what each amount actually buys

If money is the constraint, spend it in exactly this order. Each tier assumes the
previous is complete.

### €0 — do these first, this week
- Drink and store tap water in washed 5 L bottles (free)
- Write the family contact plan and print phone numbers on paper
- Learn your water shut-off, gas shut-off and consumer unit
- Download and **print** the key references (Ch. 11)
- Talk to three neighbours. This is the highest-value free action available.
- Start a compost heap; save seeds from a shop tomato/pepper
- Book a first aid course and a dental check-up

### €300 — the survival floor (L0)
Water containers 60 L · 20 L gravity filter or Sawyer-type · NaDCC tablets ·
unscented bleach · torches + headlamps + AA/AAA rechargeables + charger ·
wind-up/solar radio · 20 W solar panel + 20,000 mAh power bank · first aid kit ·
OTC medicine chest · 2 weeks of tinned/dry food · camping gas stove + 4 canisters ·
bucket toilet + coir · 2 kg soap · cash · fire extinguisher + CO alarm + smoke alarm

### €1,500 — the two-week to one-month household (L1)
Everything above plus: 400–600 L water storage · 1 month of staples in buckets +
mylar + O₂ absorbers · 100 W solar + 100 Ah LiFePO₄ + 600 W inverter ·
better stove + 2× 11 kg propane cylinders · 20 kg firewood-equivalent or a small
wood burner if you have a flue · full medicine chest + 3-month prescription buffer ·
FFP2/FFP3 masks, gloves, goggles, disinfectant · hand tools · radios

### €5,000 — the three-month isolated household (L2)
Everything above plus: 1–2 kW solar + 5 kWh LiFePO₄ + hybrid inverter ·
IBC rainwater tanks (1,000 L each) + first-flush diverter + pre-filter ·
3 months of staples for the whole household · chest freezer (A+++) ·
pressure canner + 100 jars · dehydrator · grain mill (hand + electric) ·
wood stove with cooktop + 4 m³ seasoned wood · full garden setup (beds, tools,
seeds, netting, hoses) OR full apartment kit (sprouting, microgreens, mushrooms,
fermentation) · chickens + coop + 6 months of feed

### €15,000+ — the self-renewing homestead (L3)
Everything above plus: 4–6 kW solar + 10–15 kWh storage · well with hand pump or
solar DC pump · polytunnel/greenhouse · 12 months of staples · orchard and
perennials planted · breeding stock (hens + rooster, rabbits, or goats) · 2 years
of wood + a chainsaw and safety gear + a log splitter · a proper workshop,
spares and consumables · root cellar built

> **Note the shape of this:** the first €300 buys perhaps 60% of your total
> risk reduction. The last €10,000 buys maybe 10%. Do not let the dream of a
> homestead stop you from buying a €25 water filter this weekend.

## 5. Sequencing: the correct order of operations

Within any budget, execute in this order. Each step is cheap and fast compared
to the one after it, and each makes the next one smaller.

1. **Reduce demand before adding supply.** Insulating a room, buying a thermal
   cooker, and swapping to LED lighting each cost €20–100 and permanently shrink
   your fuel, food and power needs. Adding generation to cover waste is the most
   expensive possible move. Always cut the load first.
2. **Store before you produce.** Stored food is instant, certain and cheap.
   Produced food is slow, uncertain and skill-dependent. Storage buys you the
   *time* in which production can fail and be retried.
3. **Water before food.** Always. Every time.
4. **Sanitation the same week as water.** They are the same problem.
5. **Medicine buffer before any gear.** It takes months of ordinary GP visits to
   build legally. Start the conversation now; it costs nothing today.
6. **Low tech before high tech.** A hand pump, a hand grain mill, a wood stove and
   a bucket work when electronics fail. Buy the manual version of anything
   critical *before* the powered version of anything optional.
7. **Redundancy on single points of failure only.** Two ways to get water, two
   ways to cook, two ways to make light, two ways to treat water. Not two of
   everything — that's hoarding, not planning.
8. **Skills and community last in spending, first in time.** They cost almost
   nothing but take the longest to acquire. Start them on day one, in parallel
   with everything else.

## 6. The trigger ladder — knowing *when* to act

The hardest question is not "what to buy" but "when to move". Decide the
thresholds now, in calm, and write them down. Then you act on evidence instead of
adrenaline — days before the crowds, without ever making a panic decision.

### 🟢 GREEN — normal times (this is where you are now)
**Posture:** Build to your target level. Rotate stock. Grow the garden. Train.
Nothing is urgent; everything is progressing.
**Monitor:** WHO Disease Outbreak News, your national public health agency, and
one or two boring epidemiology sources. Once a month is enough.

### 🟡 AMBER — an outbreak with sustained human-to-human transmission outside its country of origin
**This is the trigger. Not "cases in my country" — that is too late.**
**Actions, within 72 hours:**
1. **Top up everything to L2 now, in one trip.** Prices and availability are still
   normal. This is the single most valuable action in the entire plan and it
   lasts only a few days.
2. Fill every water container. Fill the car's fuel tank and all fuel cans.
3. Refill prescriptions; request early refills and a written repeat.
4. Draw out cash in small denominations.
5. Order the long-lead items you have been putting off (filters, seeds, spares,
   a battery). Delivery times collapse after this point.
6. Tell your mutual-aid group. Agree a check-in schedule.
7. Start masking in crowded indoor places and reduce optional exposure. The goal
   is to be uninfected and stocked when the wave arrives.

### 🟠 ORANGE — community transmission confirmed in your country
**Actions:**
1. Withdraw from optional public contact. Work from home if at all possible.
2. Begin the quarantine protocol on all incoming goods and people (Ch. 06).
3. Pull children out of avoidable gatherings; discuss school with your family.
4. Final gap purchases *by delivery*, not in person.
5. Start the garden cycle regardless of season — sprouts and microgreens begin
   producing in 5–14 days, indoors, any time of year (Ch. 09).
6. Set up the isolation room and PPE station before anyone is ill.
7. Switch to the crisis daily routine (Ch. 12) so it is habit before it matters.

### 🔴 RED — hospitals saturated, or utilities visibly failing, or transmission in your street
**Actions:**
1. Full household isolation. Nobody in or out without the decon protocol.
2. Switch to stored/produced resources; stop consuming anything irreplaceable.
3. Go to war-footing on hygiene: handwashing station, surface disinfection,
   isolation of any symptomatic person, ventilation maximised.
4. Daily health check for every household member (temp + pulse ox + symptoms log).
5. Light, noise and smell discipline (Ch. 10). Nothing advertises.
6. Mutual-aid group to daily radio check-in.
7. **Ration deliberately from day one**, not when stocks look low. Set the daily
   allowance based on a 12-month horizon and adjust upward later if resupply
   returns. It is far easier to relax a ration than to impose one late.

### ⚫ BLACK — utilities down indefinitely, no authority response
Full off-grid operation. Water from your controlled source only, always treated.
Sanitation to the humanure/latrine system. Warm-room only. Cooking on wood.
Security watch by rotation with the group. Preserve everything perishable
immediately. This is the level that most preparation never needs — but the
kit for it is the same kit as ORANGE, just used harder.

### Coming back down
Have de-escalation rules too, or you will live in RED for years and burn out.
Step down one level after **two weeks** of improved conditions. Restock to your
target level *before* relaxing the ration.

## 7. Test everything — the drill schedule

Nothing is real until it has been tested. Untested kit fails at exactly the wrong
moment, and the failures are always stupid: the inverter has the wrong plug, the
filter needs a fitting you don't have, nobody can light the stove, the stored
water tastes so bad the children refuse to drink it.

| Drill | Frequency | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Blackout weekend** — main breaker off, Fri night to Sun night | Twice a year, once in winter | Power, light, cooking, heat, fridge/freezer behaviour, morale |
| **Water-off day** — main stopcock closed, 24 h | Twice a year | Storage, filtration, dish washing, toilets, how much you *really* use |
| **Eat-the-store week** — only stored food, no shopping | Quarterly | Rotation, recipes, whether your family will actually eat it, gaps |
| **Isolation weekend** — nobody leaves, decon protocol active | Once a year | The protocol, PPE fit, boredom, what you forgot |
| **Cold night** — heat off, warm-room only | Once a winter | Insulation, bedding, clothing, the actual temperature |
| **Full inventory count** | Twice a year | What expired, what's missing, what you overbought |
| **Comms check** with the mutual-aid group | Monthly | Radios, phone tree, that people still care |

Keep the results in [`worksheets/drill-log.md`](../worksheets/drill-log.md).
**Every drill produces a shopping list and a fix list.** That is the point.

## 8. The fifteen mistakes that ruin plans

1. **Buying gear instead of building skills.** The gear is the easy 10%.
2. **Storing food nobody eats.** If your family won't eat it in good times, they
   won't eat it in bad times — they'll just be hungry *and* miserable. Store what
   you actually cook, and cook what you store.
3. **No water plan.** The most common and most lethal gap. Food gets all the
   attention; water kills you six times faster.
4. **Forgetting sanitation.** Second most common. You will produce 1–1.5 kg of
   waste per person per day whether or not you planned for it.
5. **Ignoring heat.** In a cold climate, heating dwarfs every other energy need.
   A 5 kWh battery is irrelevant next to a house that needs 60 kWh/day of heat.
6. **Underestimating animal feed.** Six hens eat ~300 kg of grain a year. If you
   don't store or grow it, your chickens become your competitors, then your dinner.
7. **Single points of failure.** One filter, one stove, one pair of glasses, one
   can opener, one key person who knows how it all works.
8. **Electronics with no spares.** Charge controllers and inverters fail. A spare
   €60 controller keeps a €4,000 system alive.
9. **Too much gear, no routine.** Preparation without operating cycles is a museum.
10. **OPSEC failures.** Posting the stockpile online, bragging to colleagues,
    running a loud generator. You are advertising.
11. **Planning alone.** See hard truth #3.
12. **No mental health plan.** Months of confinement, fear and boredom break
    people and marriages. Plan for morale as seriously as for calories.
13. **Waiting for certainty.** By the time it's certain, the shelves are empty.
    The AMBER trigger exists precisely to let you act early and cheaply.
14. **Never testing.** See section 7.
15. **Planning for the wrong disaster.** Pandemic ≠ war ≠ earthquake. Tactical
    gear is not a pandemic plan; a well-stocked pantry, a water system and a
    working thermometer are.

---

## 9. The one-page version

If you remember nothing else:

```
1. Fix the lowest number on your endurance audit. Repeat forever.
2. Water, sanitation, medicine, heat, food — in that order.
3. Cut demand before you add supply.
4. Store first, produce second. Storage buys time for production to fail.
5. Buy the manual version of anything critical.
6. Act at AMBER, not at RED.
7. Test it, or you don't have it.
8. Three neighbours are worth more than three thousand euros of gear.
```

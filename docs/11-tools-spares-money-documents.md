# 11 — Tools, Spares, Money & Documents

*The unglamorous chapter. It is about the things that break, the things you
cannot buy, and the pieces of paper that prove who you are.*

---

## 1. The single-point-of-failure audit

Walk through your whole plan and ask one question: **what breaks, and what happens
when it does?**

| System | The single point of failure | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Water | The filter element | 2 spare elements + chemical disinfection + a pot to boil in |
| Water | The pump | A hand pump, or a bucket and rope |
| Power | The charge controller | A spare (€60). It is the most likely electronic failure. |
| Power | The inverter | 12 V DC lighting and USB that works without it |
| Power | Cables and connectors | Spare MC4 connectors, cable, fuses, crimps |
| Cooking | The stove | A second, different-fuel stove + a thermal cooker |
| Heat | The chimney | Own rods, sweep it annually |
| Food | The can opener | Three. (The classic.) |
| Food | The grain mill | The hand version, plus spare burrs |
| Light | Batteries | Rechargeables + solar charger, one standard cell size |
| Health | Glasses | A spare pair per person + prescription copy |
| Health | Prescription medicine | A buffer, built legally over months |
| Growing | Seed | A 3–5 year seed bank + saved seed + OP varieties |
| Livestock | The rooster / breeding stock | Keep two, or an exchange agreement with the group |
| Security | The one person who knows how it all works | **Write it down and teach someone else** |
| Everything | The house burning down | Split the store; fire extinguishers; insurance documents offsite |

**That last one is the most commonly overlooked.** If only one adult understands
the power system, the water treatment, the stove and where everything is, then
that person getting ill — which is precisely what a pandemic does — disables the
whole household. **Write the manual. Teach the family. Run a drill where that
person says nothing all weekend.**

## 2. Tools

Buy quality once. Cheap tools fail at the worst moment, and there will be no
replacement.

### Hand tools — the non-negotiable core
Hammer (claw + club) · screwdrivers (or a good ratchet set with bits) ·
spanners (metric set) + adjustable · pliers (combination, long-nose, water pump) ·
wire cutters · **hacksaw + spare blades** · wood saw (panel + tenon) ·
chisels · hand drill or brace + bits (works with no power) · files and rasps ·
**sharpening stone + file + honing steel** · measuring tape · spirit level ·
square · utility knife + blades · vice or clamps · crowbar · bolt cutters ·
shovel, spade, pick · axe, splitting maul, wedges · bow saw · sledgehammer ·
wheelbarrow.

### Powered tools (while there is power)
Cordless drill/driver + 2 batteries · angle grinder + cutting/grinding discs ·
circular saw or jigsaw · chainsaw + **full PPE** · multimeter · soldering iron ·
inspection lamp.
**Standardise on one battery platform** so packs and chargers are interchangeable,
and buy a 12 V DC charger for that platform so you can charge from the solar
system directly.

### Specialist but high-value
Pipe wrench and plumbing fittings · PTFE tape · pipe freeze kit · drain rods ·
soldering/compression fittings · wire strippers and crimps · a **multimeter**
(you cannot diagnose a power system without one) · a clamp meter · a tyre pump
and puncture kit · a bicycle tool set · a sewing kit and an awl · a manual sewing
machine (treadle or hand-crank — genuinely useful and completely power-free) ·
leatherwork awl and waxed thread · a scythe and peening kit · a hand-cranked
drill · a soil auger.

## 3. The spares and consumables box

**These are the things you cannot make, cannot substitute, and will not be able
to buy.** All of it is cheap, small, and stores indefinitely. This box is one of
the highest-value €300 in the whole plan.

**Fixings and fastening:** screws and nails (assorted, in quantity) · bolts, nuts
and washers · wall plugs · **zip ties (500)** · picture wire · **galvanised wire
(a roll)** · staples · split pins · hose clamps (assorted, a box) · **jubilee
clips** · O-rings and washers (tap, hose, plumbing) · rivets.

**Tapes and adhesives:** duct/gaffer tape (5 rolls) · electrical tape ·
self-amalgamating tape (fixes leaking pipes under pressure) · PTFE tape ·
**two-part epoxy** · superglue · wood glue · contact adhesive · silicone
sealant · **plumber's putty** · **self-fusing repair tape**.

**Sealing and waterproofing:** plastic sheeting and heavy polythene · tarpaulins
(2–3, various sizes — endlessly useful) · roofing felt and nails · flashing tape ·
window film · expanding foam · bitumen paint.

**Electrical:** fuses (every rating you use — car blade, ceramic, MCBs) ·
cable (mains and DC, several gauges) · connectors and terminal blocks · crimps ·
MC4 solar connectors · **a spare MPPT charge controller** · a spare inverter if
budget allows · bulbs and LED strips · switches · a 12 V socket set · batteries
(rechargeable + a sealed pack of alkalines for the go-bag).

**Fluids:** engine oil · 2-stroke oil · chain oil · grease · WD-40/penetrating
oil · brake fluid · antifreeze · **fuel stabiliser** · methylated spirits ·
white spirit · lamp oil.

**Cordage and fabric:** rope (10 m each of 6 mm, 10 mm, plus paracord) ·
**baler twine** · bungee cords · sewing thread, needles, buttons, zips, elastic ·
**fabric patches and denim offcuts** · leather scraps · **wool blankets** ·
a roll of canvas.

**Consumables you will run out of:** matches (thousands, stored dry) and **ferro
rods (3)** · lighters (20) · candles · batteries · **water filter elements** ·
canning lids (several hundred) · pressure canner gasket and safety plug ·
salt · **soap** · **bleach/NaDCC** · sandpaper · saw blades · drill bits ·
grinding discs · chainsaw chains (2 spare) and files · **spark plugs** and a
**spare carburettor** for each small engine · air and fuel filters ·
mower/strimmer line · sharpening stones · **pencils and paper** (ink and printers
fail; you will want to write things down for a year).

**Personal:** boot laces · boot repair glue · spare work gloves (6 pairs — you
will destroy them) · spare wellies · thermal base layers · wool socks (lots) ·
a good hat.

## 4. Money and barter

### Cash
- **ATMs and card terminals fail early in every crisis** — a power cut, a network
  outage, a bank holiday, or a run on cash. **Cash works when nothing else does,
  and only for a while.**
- Hold **enough cash for 4–8 weeks of essential spending**, in **small
  denominations** (nobody will have change for a €200 note, and handing one over
  tells people exactly the wrong thing about you).
- Store it split across at least two secure places, not in an obvious box.
- Keep some smaller coins and notes in the go-bag and the car.
- Insurance: keep the policy documents where you can reach them, and photograph
  everything of value now for a future claim.

### Beyond cash
- **Hyperinflation and currency stress** are realistic in a multi-year crisis.
  Diversification — some savings in a different form (precious metal, foreign
  currency, real assets) — is a personal financial decision, not a survival
  tactic, and this plan makes no investment recommendation. If you do hold
  physical metal, **small denominations only**; nobody can make change for a
  1 oz coin with potatoes.

### Barter goods — the practical list
The best barter goods are **cheap now, impossible later, small, non-perishable,
and consumed continuously.** Buy an extra box of these purely for trade:

| Tier | Goods |
|---|---|
| **Best** | **Coffee · salt · sugar · soap · lighters and matches · batteries · painkillers · toilet paper · candles · yeast · spices · seeds · water filters · NaDCC tablets · sewing supplies · tampons/pads · condoms · alcohol (spirits — also a disinfectant and a preservative)** |
| Good | Tea · cooking oil · honey · tinned meat · rope · tape · fuel · nails and screws · socks · gloves · tools · rechargeable batteries · reading glasses · bicycle inner tubes |
| **Best of all** | **Skills.** Medical care, dentistry, repair, welding, sewing, butchery, midwifery, teaching. They cannot be stolen, they weigh nothing, and they do not run out. |
| ⚠️ Avoid | Trading **weapons, ammunition, or prescription medication** — it is illegal, it creates danger for you, and it makes you a known source |

**Barter discipline:** trade through the group and at the gate, never from your
store, never revealing quantity. A reputation as a trader attracts attention you
do not want.

## 5. The document pack

If you ever have to leave, or if you need to prove anything to anyone, this is
what matters. **Assemble it this weekend — it costs an afternoon and nothing
else.**

**One waterproof folder, grab-and-go, plus a duplicate elsewhere:**

```
IDENTITY        Passports · ID cards · driving licences · birth certificates
                Marriage certificate · residence permits · citizenship papers
PROPERTY        Deeds or lease · mortgage papers · land registry extract
                Vehicle registration and insurance
FINANCIAL       Bank and account details · insurance policies (home, health,
                life, vehicle) · pension details · recent statements · tax number
MEDICAL         For each person: prescription list (generic names + doses),
                allergies, conditions, blood group, vaccination records,
                GP and specialist contact details, EHIC/health card
                Spare glasses prescription
FAMILY          The contact plan (Ch. 10) · school records · pet vaccination
                and chip records
PRACTICAL       Photos of every room and every valuable (for insurance claims)
                Serial numbers of tools, appliances, generator, bicycles
                Your own system diagrams: power, water, stopcocks, consumer unit
                This plan, printed, with checklists filled in
CASH            Some, in the folder
```

**Three copies, three forms:**
1. **Paper** in the waterproof folder at home, where anyone in the family can find it.
2. **Encrypted USB** (VeraCrypt or similar) in the go-bag, with the passphrase
   known to two people.
3. **Encrypted cloud backup** and/or a copy with a trusted relative in another
   town. Fire, flood and theft are more likely than any pandemic scenario.

## 6. Skills — the things that take longest to acquire

**Rank your skill gaps and learn one per month.** They are free, they weigh
nothing, they cannot be stolen, and they are the difference between owning
equipment and being capable.

**Tier 1 — everyone in the household should have these**
First aid and CPR · lighting a fire in the wet · using a fire extinguisher ·
turning off water, gas and electricity at the main · basic hygiene discipline ·
cooking a meal from dry staples · hand-washing clothes · using the radio ·
where everything is and how it works.

**Tier 2 — at least one adult**
Cooking from raw ingredients including bread, beans and grains · **food
preservation: canning, fermenting, drying** · vegetable growing and seed saving ·
sharpening tools · basic sewing and mending · basic plumbing (isolate, drain,
fix a leak, replace a washer) · basic electrics and 12 V DC wiring · using a
multimeter · small-engine maintenance · bicycle repair · safe axe and saw use ·
navigation with a map and compass · water treatment · butchering a chicken or a
rabbit.

**Tier 3 — high value, worth someone in the group having**
Advanced first aid / wilderness first responder · beekeeping · dairying and
cheesemaking · larger-animal husbandry and slaughter · welding · carpentry and
building repair · soap making · brewing and distilling (for vinegar and
disinfectant — check your local law) · amateur radio · midwifery ·
dentistry basics · gunsmithing/reloading where lawful · chainsaw certification ·
tree felling.

**How to learn them:** local courses, a smallholding association, a beekeeping
club, a repair café, the person down the road who already does it. **Every one of
these skills is more fun to learn than it is to read about, and every one of them
is useful in normal times too.** That is the test of a good preparation: it makes
your ordinary life better as well.

```
[ ] Single-point-of-failure audit done; each one has an answer

[ ] Full hand-tool set, quality, with sharpening kit
[ ] Cordless platform standardised, with a 12 V charger
[ ] Multimeter owned and used
[ ] The spares box assembled (fixings, tape, epoxy, fuses, cable, cordage)
[ ] Spare charge controller, fuses, connectors and cable
[ ] Spare water filter elements ×2
[ ] Spare glasses per person
[ ] Spare canning lids, canner gasket, chainsaw chains, spark plugs, carburettor
[ ] Matches, ferro rods, lighters, candles — in quantity
[ ] Paper and pencils for a year
[ ] 4–8 weeks of cash in small denominations, split across two places
[ ] Barter box bought separately from the family store
[ ] Document pack assembled: paper + encrypted USB + offsite copy
[ ] Photos of home contents taken for insurance
[ ] System diagrams drawn and filed with the documents
[ ] Skills gap list written; one skill being learned each month
[ ] At least one adult can do every Tier 2 skill
```
